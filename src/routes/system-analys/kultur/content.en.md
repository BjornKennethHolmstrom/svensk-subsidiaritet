# Culture and media: who decides over what is shared?

## Culture is already financed at several levels

Public expenditure on culture, popular education and media was SEK 34.3 billion in 2023. The state accounted for SEK 14.9 billion, municipalities for SEK 14.2 billion and regions for SEK 5.2 billion. More than half of the money is thus decided by municipalities and regions, and part of the state’s funds is also distributed via the regions through the so-called cultural cooperation model, introduced in 2011.

The differences between regions are large. Västra Götaland Region has the highest cultural expenditure per inhabitant among the regions, and Region Stockholm the lowest. The state’s cultural expenditure as a share of total state expenditure in 2023 was the lowest since 2004.

Public service, that is Sveriges Radio, SVT and UR, has been financed since 2019 through a public service fee collected via the tax assessment. Sveriges Radio has local channels throughout the country and SVT has around twenty regional newsrooms. Since 2024 there is also a state media subsidy that, among other things, is intended to strengthen local journalism in areas with weak coverage.

## Current issues

- **A cultural canon.** A state committee presented in September 2025 the report *A Cultural Canon for Sweden* (SOU 2025:92) with 100 works and reference points. The purpose according to the terms of reference was to provide shared frames of reference. The proposal has been criticised, among others by representatives of the national minorities, who argue that they were not given a place in the work or in the result.
- **Language.** Sweden has five recognised national minorities and minority languages: Sami, Finnish, Meänkieli, Romani Chib and Yiddish, with special legal support. In addition, a large number of other languages are spoken, but Sweden does not keep official statistics on which languages are spoken. Under the Libraries Act, public libraries must pay particular attention to the national minorities and to people with a mother tongue other than Swedish.
- **Local media.** Closures of local newspapers have meant that some areas have little or no local journalistic coverage.
- **Who reaches cultural funding.** Support for culture often presupposes that one knows the system, can write applications and has a network. This can make it harder for new groups and non-professionals to receive support.

## The cybernetic reading

Cultural life contains an almost unlimited variation: of expressions, languages, traditions and audiences. No single body can assess everything. Swedish cultural policy already handles this in several ways:

- **The arm’s-length principle:** politicians decide on the framework, while the assessment of individual applications is made by experts. This is a way to strengthen assessment capacity by distributing it.
- **Several levels and several funders:** municipalities, regions, the state, popular education, associational life and private funders give artists and associations several possible routes.
- **Common frameworks:** certain things, such as support for national minorities’ languages or the mission of libraries, are regulated nationally so that they apply everywhere.

The question is not whether culture is centrally controlled, because it is so to a limited extent, but whether the different routes are sufficiently open and whether certain groups, places or expressions systematically fall through the cracks.

## Subsidiarity-based proposals

The proposals are ideas to test in limited pilots with evaluation. Each proposal has its own page.

| Proposal | Brief description |
|--------|------------------|
| [Grants for everyday culture](./vardagskultur) | Small, simple grants for non-professional cultural creation, with a simplified application and local assessment. |
| [Local public service](./hyperlokal-media) | Support for local, editorially independent media in areas with weak coverage, as a complement to Sveriges Radio and SVT. |
| [Language communities](./sprakgemenskaper) | Support for cultural creation in languages other than Swedish, with advisory groups from the language communities. |
| [Cultural commons](./kulturella-allmanningar) | Cultural centres and community venues where associations and residents gain more influence over the programme and access to premises and equipment. |
| [Cultural heritage in school](./kanon) | Working methods in which pupils explore both a shared cultural heritage and traditions in their own family and local environment. |

## Objections

| Objection | How the proposals need to address it |
|---------|------|
| Quality declines | The proposals complement professional cultural life and do not take money from it. |
| Shared frames of reference are weakened | The proposals on school and cultural heritage should cover both the shared and the local, not replace one with the other. |
| Support by language can divide society | Support for cultural creation in other languages should be combined with translation and meetings between languages and audiences. |
| Local media can become biased | Editorial independence, a responsible publisher and scrutiny under the same rules as for other media. |

## Conclusion

Swedish culture is already financed more than half locally and regionally, and the state’s funds are partly distributed via the regions. The claim that cultural policy is governed from Stockholm is therefore not true in its simple form. The real questions concern who reaches the existing forms of support, how languages and traditions outside the majority culture are given space, and how local journalism can remain where the market does not sustain it. The proposals here test opening more routes in, rather than moving decisions from one level to another.

### Three things you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Find out who in the municipality applies for and receives cultural funding, and who does not.

**If you work at a library or in a cultural activity:** Investigate how the activity reaches those who speak the national minority languages and languages other than Swedish.

**If you are a resident:** Find out what premises and forms of support exist for associations and cultural creators in your municipality.
