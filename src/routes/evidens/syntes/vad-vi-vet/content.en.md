# What We Know

## Claims the corpus currently supports with reasonable confidence

This page presents only claims for which the evidence currently supports a reasonably confident synthesis — not everything we suspect, hope, or find plausible. Each claim states its scope and confidence explicitly. See the full picture, including everything *not* listed here, in the **[Evidence Matrix →](/evidens/syntes/matris/)**.

---

### C1 — Governance fit

> **Current evidence is more consistent with a conditional governance-fit proposition than with a universal decentralisation principle.**

**Scope:** Swedish territorial and administrative reforms studied so far (municipal mergers, school decentralization, elder care, fiscal equalization, intermunicipal cooperation).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/)–S5, hypotheses H3, H5, H7, H8, H9, H14.
**Qualification:** This is a pattern across five cases in one country's administrative history — not yet a cross-national or cross-domain generalization.

---

### C2 — Formal decentralization is an insufficient proxy for subsidiarity

> **Formal transfer of responsibility does not reliably establish substantive decision proximity.**

**Scope:** Cases where formal responsibility moved (school municipalization, municipal mergers).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), S2, hypotheses H4, H10.
**Qualification:** We have not yet completed a full authority/information/resource mapping below the formal administrative level for any case — this claim rests on partial reconstruction.

---

### C3 — Functional differentiation matters

> **The appropriate governance scale appears to vary substantially by function.**

**Scope:** Swedish municipal functions examined in S1, S3, S4, S5.
**Confidence:** Medium–High.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), S3, S4, S5, hypotheses H5, H7, H14.
**Qualification:** This is one of our best-supported claims — even the state's own 1961 reform documents explicitly recognized different functional scale requirements — but it still needs systematic cross-sector comparison rather than case-by-case observation.

---

### C4a — Governance functions can be factorized across scales

> **Different components of a governance function — political authority, production, specialist capacity, financing, and coordination — can sit at different scales rather than being bundled at a single territorial level.**

**Scope:** Comparison of merger (S1) against cooperation (S5) and equalization (S4), plus the eight municipal functions component-coded in the [governance factorization test](/evidens/datamodeller/simuleringar/styrningsfaktorisering/).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** S4, S5, comparison with [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), hypotheses H8, H9, plus the component coding of IT, procurement, environmental inspection, fire and rescue, water and sewerage, physical planning, elder care, and specialist social services.
**Qualification:** Upgraded from "promising" to conditionally supported after component separation was observed in all eight functions. But the *existence* of an architecture says nothing about its outcomes — a direct, controlled comparison of merger vs. cooperation vs. independent provision across the same functions is still missing.

*The other half of the old C4 — that problem structure suffices to predict **which** factorization is right — is now **C4b** and stands as mixed/unresolved in the [Evidence Matrix](/evidens/syntes/matris/). It doesn't belong on this page yet.*

---

### C5 — Coordination is a design variable, not merely a cost

> **When authority and capacity are distributed across multiple nodes, coordination architecture becomes a critical determinant of whether decentralisation produces benefits or fragmentation.**

**Scope:** Cases involving cross-boundary or cross-principal coordination (S3, S5).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** S3, S5, hypotheses H6, H8, H14, and GAE's formal coordination mechanisms (used as an [IP] interpretive lens).
**Qualification:** GAE's coordination-tax formalism is a model result, not independently empirically validated in these Swedish cases yet.

---

## What's deliberately absent from this list

Seven claims (C4b and C6, and hypotheses H2, H11–H13, H15–H20) are currently **exploratory or unresolved** — not because they're uninteresting, but because the evidence doesn't yet support confident synthesis. They live in the **[Evidence Matrix](/evidens/syntes/matris/)** and **[What We Don't Know](/evidens/syntes/okanda/)** instead of here.

We'd rather have a short, defensible list than a long, overconfident one.
