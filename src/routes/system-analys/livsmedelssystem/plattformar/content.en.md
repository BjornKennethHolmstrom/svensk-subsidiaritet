# Digital infrastructure for direct sales

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Open digital tools for ordering, payment and pickup, which can be used by REKO rings, farm shops and producer cooperatives. The tools are owned and run cooperatively or by a non-profit organisation, so that they cannot be bought up and controlled by a single actor.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

The REKO rings, which originated in Finland and have spread to many Swedish towns, show that there is an interest in buying directly from producers. Orders are usually placed in Facebook groups and the goods are collected at a set time and place. The model works, but depends on a commercial platform and is labour-intensive for the administrators.

## How it would work

- **Open source and open standards**, so that several instances can be run by different cooperatives, associations or municipalities and can communicate with one another.
- **Direct payment** between buyer and producer, for example via Swish, so that the platform does not handle customers’ money.
- **Pickup points**, for example at farms, in association premises or at libraries.
- **Ownership and governance** in an economic association or foundation, with statutes that make it difficult to change the purpose or sell the operation.

## On cooperatives and buyouts

The cooperative form is no guarantee that an operation remains small-scale or local. Lantmännen is an example: it is still an economic association owned by farmers, but has grown into a large group. If the aim is to retain a local and open mission, that needs to be written into the statutes.

## On public procurement

Earlier versions of this page proposed that municipalities should require suppliers to use the platform and that “local” should be defined in law for procurements. That is not compatible with the procurement rules, which prohibit suppliers being favoured because of where they are located. The platform should instead be built for direct sales to households and for coordination between producers.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Development of an open platform, and pilot operation together with some REKO rings and producer groups in different parts of the country over two years.

**Evaluation:** number of producers and buyers, sales, administrative time for the rings’ administrators, and whether the operation can cover its own costs after the pilot.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost |
|------|---------|
| Development of platform | approx. SEK 2–3 million, one-off cost |
| Operation and support during the pilot | approx. SEK 0.5–1 million per year |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million |

## Objections

**“No one wants yet another app.”** Therefore the platform should be simple and usable in a browser.

**“The chains can start their own equivalents.”** They can. The aim is not to outcompete them, but for there to be an open channel owned by the producers and buyers themselves.

**“It will never account for more than a small share of sales.”** Probably not. But even a small channel can be crucial for small-scale producers.
