# Competence banks

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A local brokerage, for example via the municipality or region, where professionals, pensioners and others with special knowledge can offer guest contributions in school: a workshop, a study visit or a series of lessons together with a teacher. The aim is to make it easier for schools to find the right person, and for knowledgeable people to get in contact with schools.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Schools already invite guest lecturers and make study visits, but this often happens through personal contacts. Schools in areas with weak networks may find it harder to find people to invite. At the same time, there are many who would gladly share their knowledge but do not know how to get in contact with schools.

## What the rules say

Earlier versions of this page claimed that it is in principle impossible for people without teacher certification to teach in school. That is not correct in that form:

- **Guest contributions** from people without certification are permitted as a complement to the teacher’s teaching.
- **Uncertified teachers** can be employed under certain conditions, for example when certified teachers are lacking.
- **Vocational subjects** in upper secondary school do not require teacher certification in the same way as other subjects.
- **Grades**, however, may only be set by certified teachers, and a **criminal record check** is required for those who work in school.

## How it would work

- **A broker** in the municipality or region receives expressions of interest from knowledgeable people, checks references and ensures that a criminal record check is carried out.
- **Schools** can search among the knowledgeable people and book guest contributions.
- **The teacher** plans the element, is present and is responsible for the teaching.
- **Remuneration** is paid according to the school’s usual routines for fees.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three to five municipalities or regions over three years.

**Evaluation:** number of brokered contributions, which schools use them, teachers’ and pupils’ experience, and who offers their knowledge.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Per municipality or region and year |
|------|--------------------------------|
| Broker (0.5–1 FTE) | approx. SEK 0.3–0.6 million |
| Fees to knowledgeable people | approx. SEK 0.2 million |
| Simple digital service and evaluation | approx. SEK 0.2 million |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Criminal record check:** Those who work in school with children must provide an extract from the criminal record.
2. **Personal data:** The brokerage handles personal data and must comply with data protection rules.
3. **Tax and fees:** Fees are taxed according to ordinary rules.

## Objections

**“It replaces teachers with cheaper labour.”** Guest contributions should complement, not replace, teachers’ teaching.

**“Quality varies.”** The teacher assesses which elements are suitable, and the broker collects feedback.

**“There are already contacts between school and working life.”** Yes, and the brokerage should build on them, especially for schools that lack their own networks.

## Swedish experience to build on

- **Study associations**, with long experience of organising activities with leaders without formal pedagogical training.
- **Sports association leaders**, who every week lead children and young people on a voluntary basis.
- **Existing cooperation between school and working life**, for example within vocational programmes.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Propose a trial broker, who maps out who wants to contribute and which schools want to receive them.

**If you are professionally active or a pensioner:** Contact a nearby school and offer a guest contribution.
