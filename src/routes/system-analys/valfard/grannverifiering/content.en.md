# Neighbour verification

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A simplified and faster processing of small, urgent needs within social assistance, for example a broken refrigerator or an unexpected expense. The applicant submits a self-declaration of the need and can, if they wish, name a neighbour or another person who can confirm it. The cases are followed up with spot checks afterwards.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

The processing of social assistance often requires extensive documentation: bank statements, receipts and certificates. This is justified when large amounts and long-term support are being assessed, but can be disproportionate for a small, clear and urgent need. In acute emergencies, social services can already make rapid decisions today, but for needs that are not acute in the strict sense, processing can take longer.

## How it would work

**Simplified procedure for small amounts (proposal: maximum SEK 5,000):**

1. The applicant describes the need in a simple application and submits a self-declaration that the information is correct.
2. Social services make a quick assessment based on what they already know about the applicant.
3. The applicant can, if they wish, name a person who can confirm the need, for example a neighbour, a friend or someone in an association. It is voluntary and must never be a requirement.
4. A decision is made within a short time, preferably a few working days.
5. Some of the cases are reviewed afterwards. Incorrect information leads to recovery and, in serious cases, a police report, as today.

**Limitations:** The procedure is used only for limited amounts and a limited number of times per year. Those who do not want to or cannot use it receive ordinary processing.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Five to ten municipalities over two years.

**Evaluation:** processing time, proportion of incorrect payments in the spot checks compared with ordinary processing, applicants’ experience and how much time is freed up for case officers.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost per year (10 municipalities) |
|------|----------------------------------|
| Adaptation of e-service and routines | approx. SEK 1 million first year |
| Spot checks | approx. SEK 1 million |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million |

## Legal issues

1. **Confidentiality:** The fact that someone is applying for social assistance is covered by social services confidentiality. If social services contact a neighbour, they disclose information about the applicant. This requires the applicant’s explicit consent, and the contact should be limited to the absolute minimum necessary. Earlier versions of this page claimed that there was no confidentiality problem. That was wrong.
2. **Duty to investigate:** Social services must investigate the need to the extent required. How far a simplified procedure can go within the law needs to be investigated.
3. **Data protection:** Information about the person confirming the need is personal data and must be handled accordingly.

## Objections

**“Neighbours should not know that someone is applying for benefits.”** This is the strongest objection, and the reason why the possibility of naming a neighbour is voluntary. Many who apply for social assistance do not, for good reason, want neighbours to know. If the pilot shows that almost no one uses the possibility, or that it is experienced as pressure, it should be removed and only the self-declaration retained.

**“Social pressure and dependency.”** Someone who depends on another person to receive support can end up in a vulnerable situation, for example in relationships involving control or violence. Therefore, confirmation from another person must never be a requirement.

**“It will lead to more fraud.”** The spot checks should show whether the proportion of incorrect payments increases. The amounts are limited.

**“Those without a network are disadvantaged.”** The self-declaration should be sufficient, so that no one is disadvantaged because they lack someone who can attest.

## What you can do

**If you are a unit manager in social services:** Review which documentation is required for small one-off amounts, and whether some can be replaced with a self-declaration and spot checks.

**If you are a municipal politician:** Propose a pilot with simplified processing of small amounts, with evaluation of both processing time and incorrect payments.
