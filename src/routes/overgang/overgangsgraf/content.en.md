# The Transition Graph

## A capability-based path, not a straight line

The naive picture of reform is a straight line:

> Sweden → decentralization → polycentrism → GGF

The Transition Graph replaces that picture with something more robust:

> **Sweden → pluralism + epistemic capacity + coordination + accountability → selective decentralization → integrated polycentricity → meta-governance**

The core rule is:

> **Distributed authority must not grow faster than the system's ability to coordinate, maintain accountability, share knowledge, and resolve conflicts across institutional boundaries.**

---

## What should initially be preserved

The transition should **not** deliberately weaken:

- crisis response capacity
- budgetary coherence
- legal accountability
- administrative competence
- national coordination
- clear responsibility for executive decisions

These are valuable capabilities that later architectures must reproduce through different means before they can safely be reduced.

## Five branches

### A. Political decentering

From coalition dependence to parliamentary pluralism: stronger committee capacity, normalized issue-by-issue majorities, a clearer separation between confidence questions and ordinary legislation.

**Gain:** political pluralism without executive collapse. **Risk:** coalition instability and policy incoherence. **Gate before moving further:** Parliament must demonstrably produce functioning majorities on individual policy areas without systemic paralysis.

### B. Epistemic decentering

Can begin before any formal authority moves. A national evidence function (synthesis, forecasting, systems analysis, scenario modelling), permanent citizens'-assembly capacity, and clear channels for Parliament to refer complex issues to deliberation.

**Gain:** deliberative legitimacy and epistemic diversity. **Risk:** citizens' assemblies becoming either decorative or a de facto unelected government. **Gate:** their constitutional role must remain differentiated from elected legislative authority.

### C. Accountability decentering

Perhaps the most important bridge branch. Explicit mandates (mandate → authority → responsibility → measurable targets → reporting requirements), public decision provenance, independent audit, clear paths for appeal and escalation.

**Gain:** localized authority without responsibility diffusion — the difference between "nobody is in charge" and "the right node is in charge, and we can say exactly who."

### D. Coordination decentering

The branch that prevents the classic transition valley. Cross-sector coordination councils, shared data and modelling infrastructure, regular cross-sector scenario exercises — all before anything substantial is decentralized.

**Gain:** the state learns to coordinate **networks** of institutions, not just ministries.

### E. Interoperability

An easily overlooked but decisive layer: common standards for data exchange, decision records, jurisdiction boundaries, mutual recognition, emergency escalation and shared metrics. This is effectively the **network protocol layer** for polycentric governance.

## The phase sequence, briefly

**Phase 0 — preserve core capacity.** Don't weaken crisis preparedness, administrative competence, legal accountability or budgetary integrity.

**Phase 1 — build intelligence.** Evidence infrastructure, policy experimentation, ex-post evaluation, systems analysis.

**Phase 2 — build deliberative capacity.** Citizens' assemblies, better parliamentary deliberation, structured stakeholder participation.

**Phase 3 — build network capacity.** Cross-ministry coordination, cross-level coordination, shared data, interoperability.

**Phase 4 — build distributed accountability.** Mandate clarity, decision provenance, audit, appeal, conflict resolution.

**Phase 5 — begin selective decentralization.** Only now: subsidiarity reviews, pilots, authority transfer within bounded domains.

**Phase 6 — expand polycentricity.** Functional regions, bioregional arrangements, nested governance.

**Phase 7 — institutionalize meta-governance.** Continuously: monitor → evaluate → redesign → test → adapt.

Note that the phases partly run in parallel — phases 1–4 in particular can proceed simultaneously, since they build different capabilities that later gates (phase 5 onward) require in combination.
