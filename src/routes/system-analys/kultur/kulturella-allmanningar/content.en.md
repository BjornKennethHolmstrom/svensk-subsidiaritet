# Cultural commons

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Cultural centres and community venues where associations and residents gain greater influence over the activities: part of the programme budget is distributed according to proposals from users, premises can be booked cheaply or free of charge, and equipment can be borrowed. Operations can be run by the municipality or handed over to an association, foundation or cooperative.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Many municipal cultural centres and libraries already have a broad offering and cooperate with associations. But the programme is often decided by employees, and it can be difficult for new groups to gain access to premises, stages and equipment. At the same time, Sweden has a long tradition of venues owned and run by associations, such as village halls, People’s Houses and People’s Parks.

## How it would work

- **Part of the programme budget is distributed according to proposals from users**, for example via an open meeting or a digital vote. The rest is planned by the staff.
- **Premises and equipment available to associations and residents**, at low or no cost, with simple booking rules.
- **The staff’s role shifts** towards supporting others in carrying out their ideas, with technology, marketing and contacts.
- **Governance:** the municipality retains responsibility, or hands over operations to an association, foundation or cooperative according to an agreement.

## Legal conditions

Municipalities can already today hand over the management of a municipal activity to an association, foundation or other private operator, as long as it does not involve exercise of public authority. No amendment to the Local Government Act is therefore needed, which earlier versions of this page claimed. However, procurement rules and rules on state aid may need to be considered depending on how the agreement is designed. The state also provides support to public community venues via the National Board of Housing, Building and Planning.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Five to ten cultural centres or community venues in different types of municipalities over three years. The locations are chosen together with the municipalities and local associations.

**Evaluation:** how many and who use the building, how many events are carried out by associations and residents, how residents experience their influence and how staff experience their new role.

## Rough cost estimate

Operations are paid for as today by the municipality. Additional costs mainly concern equipment, staff training and evaluation, proposed at a few hundred thousand kronor per building per year.

## Objections

**“Certain groups take over.”** Clear rules for booking and distribution, and staff who actively invite those who do not contact them themselves, are needed.

**“Quality declines.”** Part of the programme is still planned by the staff.

**“Staff lose their role.”** The role changes, and the change needs to take place in dialogue with the employees and their trade unions.

## Experience to build on

- **Village halls, People’s Houses and People’s Parks**, association-run venues with a long tradition.
- **Fryshuset in Stockholm**, which is run as a foundation with great influence for young people.
- **Libraries’ makerspaces**, where equipment can be borrowed and used.
- **Study associations**, which already support associations’ cultural activities.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Propose that part of the programme budget in a cultural centre be distributed according to proposals from users, as a trial.

**If you work in a cultural centre:** Review how easy it is for associations and individuals to book premises and borrow equipment.

**If you are active in an association:** Contact the municipality’s cultural administration or a village hall or People’s House association about premises.
