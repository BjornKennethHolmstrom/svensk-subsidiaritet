# Locally owned electricity grids

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A review of experiences from electricity grids owned by municipalities and economic associations, and a proposal to investigate whether more local grids can be run in such forms. Grid fees are regulated by the Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate regardless of who owns the grid, but the form of ownership affects who shares in the surplus and who has influence.

*This is a proposal to investigate and test. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## What applies today

- **Electricity grids are natural monopolies.** The customer cannot choose a grid company.
- **The revenue of grid companies is regulated.** The Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate determines how much revenue each grid company may charge during a period, a so-called revenue framework. Grid companies thus do not set their fees freely. Earlier versions of this page described it as grid companies being able to charge high fees without limitation.
- **Ownership varies.** In addition to the three large grid companies, there are a large number of smaller grid companies, many of which are owned by municipalities and some by economic associations in which the customers are members.

Locally and cooperatively owned electricity grids are thus nothing new in Sweden.

## The proposal

1. **A mapping** of how municipally and cooperatively owned grids differ from others in terms of fees, quality, investments and customer influence, using the Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate’s statistics.
2. **An investigation** of the conditions for municipalities or cooperatives to take over local grids, for example when a grid area is sold.
3. **Local influence** in existing grid companies, for example through customer councils.

## Legal issues

1. **Grid concession:** Operating an electricity grid requires a grid concession. Transfer of a concession is assessed by the Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate.
2. **Property protection:** A compulsory takeover of a grid from an owner would require compensation and strong legal support.
3. **Competence:** Operating an electricity grid requires technical competence and preparedness for disruptions, for example during storms.

## Objections

**“Small grid companies have higher costs.”** That may be true, since there are economies of scale. The mapping should show how it looks.

**“Cooperative grids have no profit requirement, so fees become lower.”** The revenue frameworks apply to all grid companies. However, any surplus in a cooperative can be returned to the members.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Find out who owns the electricity grid in the municipality and how the grid fees compare with those of neighbouring municipalities.
