# Authority Depth

**Status: proposed / not yet validated.** Defined here as a working framework for future data collection, not yet applied systematically to any case with real data.

---

## What does it measure?

How far *effective* decision-making authority actually travels below a formal administrative boundary — as distinct from how far *responsibility* was formally assigned. This is the measure the corpus's "nested centralization" hypothesis (H4) needs in order to move from a case-by-case qualitative judgment to something comparable across cases.

## Why measure it?

Every case built so far shows the same gap: a reform formally assigns authority to a level (state → municipality, municipality → school, municipality → local organ), but the actual exercise of discretion, initiative and resource control often stops at an intermediate point. **[S2](/evidens/fall/sverige/skoldecentralisering/)** shows this most starkly — the reform explicitly intended authority to reach individual schools and professionals, but evidence indicates local steering below the municipal level was uneven, especially in smaller municipalities. Without a measure of *how far down* authority actually reached, this remains a directional finding rather than a comparable one.

## Conceptual definition

Authority depth is the number of institutional levels, counted from the level formally granted responsibility, at which meaningful discretion over a decision still genuinely exists — as opposed to the level at which the decision has effectively already been made and lower levels are only implementing it.

## Proposed operational definition

For a given function and formal responsibility chain (e.g., state → municipality → school → teacher), code at each level whether that level has: (a) legal authority to decide, (b) actual practiced discretion (evidenced by documented variation in decisions across otherwise similar units), (c) control over the resources needed to act on that discretion, and (d) the ability to initiate a decision rather than only respond to one. Authority depth is the lowest level at which most of (a)–(d) hold.

## Units

An ordinal level count along the specific institutional chain being examined — not comparable across chains of different lengths without normalization, which is an open methodological question.

## Data sources (proposed)

Legal/regulatory text defining formal responsibility (for a); documented decision variation across comparable units, such as the school-plan analysis used in [S2](/evidens/fall/sverige/skoldecentralisering/) (for b); budget and resource-allocation data (for c); process documentation showing who raises an issue versus who responds to one (for d).

## Known limitations

- (a)–(d) may not move together — a level can have legal authority without practiced discretion, or discretion without resource control (see [S2](/evidens/fall/sverige/skoldecentralisering/)'s finding that formal responsibility for outcomes was paired with weak resource-and-need-based allocation in practice).
- Coding "documented variation" as evidence of discretion requires a credible baseline for what variation would look like *without* genuine local discretion — not yet established.
- This measure is likely to require substantial qualitative judgment even after operationalization; it is not intended to become a purely mechanical index.

## Illustrative (not yet measured) example

**[Case S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/)**'s 1979 local-organs reform is a useful test case for this measure once operationalized: local organs had (a) some legal authority within a scope set by the council, but the council retained control over (c) resources and (d) initiative for referring matters upward — a textbook case of authority depth stopping short of the nominal delegation.

## Related hypotheses

**H4** (nested centralization), **H1** (local information use requires actual discretion, not just formal responsibility), **H3** (capacity).

## Related GAE concepts

Used as an [IP] interpretive framework, not empirically validated by this measure yet: **GAE XII/XVIII** (boundary design and boundary dynamics — authority depth is essentially asking where a governance boundary is *functionally* located, as distinct from where it's *formally* drawn).
