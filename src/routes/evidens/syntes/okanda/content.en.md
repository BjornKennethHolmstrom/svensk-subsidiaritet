# What We Don't Know

## A feature, not a weakness

Writing "there is currently insufficient evidence to answer this" is one of the most useful things a research programme can do. This page documents unresolved questions and evidence gaps explicitly, rather than letting them hide inside vague or overconfident language elsewhere on the site.

---

## Highest-priority gaps

### 1. Direct measures of actual decision proximity

**Why it matters:** Almost everything on this site depends on distinguishing formal decentralization from actual decision proximity, but we don't yet have a validated measure of the latter.
**Current evidence:** Case-level qualitative reconstruction only (e.g. [Case S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/)).
**Why unresolved:** No operational measure has been built or tested yet.
**Needed evidence:** A working "Decision Proximity" measure — see the planned page under [Data & Models](/evidens/datamodeller/).
**Priority:** High.

### 2. Authority/information/resource mapping below formal administrative levels

**Why it matters:** Hypothesis H4 (nested centralization) depends on knowing what actually happens inside a municipality, not just what happens between the state and the municipality.
**Current evidence:** Partial, for S1 and S2 only.
**Why unresolved:** This requires granular administrative and organizational data that hasn't yet been assembled.
**Needed evidence:** Municipality → district/institution → professional → individual authority maps for at least the five core cases.
**Priority:** High.

### 3. Comparison of merger vs. cooperation vs. independent provision

**Why it matters:** Claim C4 ("scale can be factorized") depends on this comparison, and it's directly policy-relevant.
**Current evidence:** Separate case evidence for merger (S1) and cooperation (S5), but no controlled comparison across the same functions.
**Why unresolved:** No study in our corpus directly compares all three arrangements for the same municipal function.
**Needed evidence:** A comparative study or dataset covering equivalent functions under each arrangement.
**Priority:** High.

### 4. Longitudinal evidence on adaptation and institutional learning

**Why it matters:** Hypothesis H15 (adaptive subsidiarity) and the broader Governance Architecture's meta-governance layer both assume institutions can detect and correct misallocations of authority over time.
**Current evidence:** Suggestive examples (S1's 1979 local-organs correction; S3's repeated boundary adjustments; S4's iterative formula revision), but no systematic before/after outcome evidence.
**Why unresolved:** These are pattern observations, not causal tests of whether the corrections actually improved outcomes.
**Needed evidence:** Outcome data spanning a correction event, ideally with a comparison group.
**Priority:** Medium–High.

### 5. Fiscal equalization effects on service outcomes and incentives

**Why it matters:** Claim C1 depends partly on H9 (autonomy + equalization can coexist), but coexistence isn't the same as good incentive design.
**Current evidence:** S4 documents that the system exists and operates; incentive and formula effects are noted as a limitation, not tested.
**Why unresolved:** No dedicated incentive-effects study in the current corpus.
**Needed evidence:** Analysis of how equalization formulas affect local fiscal behavior over time.
**Priority:** Medium.

### 6. Swedish evidence on participation and capability development (R8)

**Why it matters:** Hypotheses H16–H20 depend on this, and R8 is currently the most exploratory research topic on the site.
**Current evidence:** International literature (OECD civic-education synthesis; a 2025 meta-analysis of democratic-innovation effects) shows some support for participation improving knowledge, internal political efficacy and reasoning — but no dedicated Swedish case yet.
**Why unresolved:** The R8 empirical corpus (planned cases S11–S12) has not been built.
**Needed evidence:** Swedish citizens'-assembly or deliberative-participation case studies with before/after capability measures.
**Priority:** Medium.

### 7. Operationalization of systems reasoning

**Why it matters:** Hypothesis H19 is plausible and GAE-relevant, but "systems reasoning" risks becoming an unfalsifiable, self-confirming construct if poorly operationalized.
**Current evidence:** None specific to this project; general literature on the construct is thin and contested.
**Why unresolved:** No validated, independent measure currently exists that we're confident in adopting.
**Needed evidence:** A robust, pre-existing measurement instrument, evaluated on its own merits before use.
**Priority:** Medium (methodologically important, not urgent).

### 8. Direct tests of architecture × capability interaction

**Why it matters:** Hypothesis H20, and the whole "governance effectiveness = architecture × capability" framing, is currently a conceptual model, not an empirically tested one.
**Current evidence:** Conceptual only, drawing on S2/S3/S5 observations plus the R8 framework.
**Why unresolved:** This requires genuinely comparative data — same architecture, varying capability, or vice versa — that doesn't yet exist in the corpus.
**Needed evidence:** A comparative design isolating architecture from capability effects.
**Priority:** Medium–Low (important but requires the other gaps to close first).
