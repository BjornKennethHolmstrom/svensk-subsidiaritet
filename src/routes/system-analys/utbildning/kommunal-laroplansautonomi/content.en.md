# Local profiles in teaching

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A trial in which a number of schools or municipalities are given greater scope to link parts of teaching to local conditions, for example local business life, nature, cultural heritage or language, while the national knowledge requirements and grades apply as usual.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Pupils in different parts of the country grow up under different conditions. Teaching that connects to pupils’ local environment can increase motivation and make knowledge more useful. At the same time, it is important that all pupils receive the same basic knowledge and that grades mean the same thing across the whole country.

## What already exists

Swedish schools already have a certain local scope:

- **The curricula** state goals and core content but not working methods, order or teaching materials. That is decided by the teachers and the school.
- **The school’s choice and the pupil’s choice** in compulsory school provide scope for local priorities and deeper study.
- **Programme specialisations and individual choices** in upper secondary school can be adapted to local conditions.
- **Sami and other national minority languages**, as well as mother tongue instruction, are adapted to pupils’ needs.

Earlier versions of this page claimed that the curriculum controls content and order in detail and that Sweden is unusually centralised compared with neighbouring countries. That is not correct in that form. In Finland, however, there is a clearer model with national core curricula that each municipality supplements with its own curriculum, which could be a model.

## How it would work

1. **National knowledge requirements and grades** apply as usual in all subjects.
2. **A local profile** is developed by the municipality or school together with teachers, pupils, guardians and local actors. The profile describes how teaching is linked to local conditions, for example through recurring projects, study visits or interdisciplinary themes.
3. **Expanded scope** for the school’s choice or equivalent in the trial, proposed at up to ten percent of teaching time, for teaching linked to the profile.
4. **The Swedish Schools Inspectorate** follows up that pupils meet the knowledge requirements.

**What we know:** The curricula state goals and core content but not working methods and order. The school’s choice and the pupil’s choice already provide a certain local scope. Expanded scope likely requires amendments to the Education Ordinance, and the proposal is therefore exploratory.

**Most important assumption:** That teaching linked to local conditions increases pupils’ motivation and learning, without worsening their results against the national knowledge requirements.

**What we do not know:** How it affects equivalence, which has already deteriorated. The evaluation should particularly monitor pupils with weaker circumstances.

- **Stop if:** pupils’ results in the subjects become worse than in comparable schools.
- **Adjust if:** motivation increases but results do not change; then the scope may need to be used differently.
- **Continue if:** results are at least as good as in comparable schools, and pupils’ motivation and attendance are higher.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Five to ten municipalities or schools of different types over four to five years.

**Evaluation:** results in subjects compared with similar schools, pupils’ motivation and attendance, transition to further studies and work, and teachers’ experience.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Per pilot |
|------|-----------|
| Development of profile | approx. SEK 0.3 million |
| In-service training for teachers | approx. SEK 0.5–1 million |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million |

## Legal issues

1. **Timetable and teaching time:** Expanded local scope likely requires amendments to the Education Ordinance or a special decision on pilot activity.
2. **Independent schools:** The trial should be open to both municipal and independent schools on equal terms.

## Objections

**“Equivalence is threatened.”** This is the most important objection, especially since equivalence has already deteriorated. Therefore national knowledge requirements apply fully, and the scope is limited.

**“Local content may become too narrow.”** Pupils should be able to move, study and work anywhere. The profile should be a complement, not a reason to close doors.

**“Teachers do not have time.”** Time needs to be allocated for developing the profile.

## What you can do

**If you are a teacher or head teacher:** Review how the school uses the school’s choice and other local spaces today.

**If you are a municipal politician:** Find out how schools cooperate with local working life and associational life.
