# Portfolios as assessment material

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Pupils collect project work, practical demonstrations and reflections in a portfolio, which is used as one of several bases when the teacher sets grades. A number of higher education institutions are also testing portfolios within the framework of the alternative selection for certain programmes.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Written tests are an effective way to assess certain knowledge, but capture less well abilities such as carrying out a longer project, collaborating or solving practical problems. At the same time, grades are important for selection to upper secondary school and higher education, and they must be fair and comparable.

## What already exists

- **The teacher sets grades** based on all available information about the pupil’s knowledge, not only tests. Portfolios can already be used as a basis today.
- **The upper secondary project** is a larger, independent piece of work in all upper secondary programmes.
- **Alternative selection:** Higher education institutions can already admit some students to a programme through selection criteria other than grades and the university entrance exam, for example aptitude tests or interviews.

## How it would work

- **The portfolio** contains project work, practical demonstrations, reflections and documentation of longer work.
- **The teacher assesses and sets grades**, with the portfolio as one basis among several. National tests are used as support for assessment.
- **External readers**, for example from working life or higher education, can give feedback on the portfolios, but do not take part in grading.
- **Co-assessment** between teachers in the pilot should make assessment more equivalent.

**To higher education:** Some higher education institutions are testing portfolios as a selection basis within the alternative selection for selected programmes, and are following up how admitted students manage their studies.

**What we know:** Under the Education Act, grades are set by certified teachers, who already today may use all available information about the pupil’s knowledge. Higher education institutions can admit some students through alternative selection.

**Most important assumption:** That portfolios give the teacher a better basis for assessing abilities that tests capture poorly, without making assessment less equivalent.

- **Stop if:** co-assessment shows that teachers assess the same portfolios very differently and that this cannot be corrected.
- **Adjust if:** assessment is equivalent but takes an unreasonable amount of time.
- **Continue if:** assessment is as equivalent as other grading, and pupils demonstrate abilities that do not emerge from the tests.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Ten schools and two or three higher education institutions over four years.

**Evaluation:** pupils’ motivation and results, equivalence in assessment between teachers and schools, teachers’ workload, and how students admitted via portfolio manage their studies compared with others.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost |
|------|---------|
| In-service training and co-assessment for teachers | approx. SEK 2–3 million per year |
| Coordination at the schools | approx. SEK 1 million per year |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 1.5 million per year |

## Legal issues

1. **Grading:** Under the Education Act, grades are set by certified teachers. Peers and external readers therefore cannot form part of the grade. Earlier versions of this page proposed that, which is not compatible with the law.
2. **Admission:** Portfolios as a selection basis should be tested within the current rules for alternative selection before any changes are considered.
3. **Personal data:** Portfolios with images and films contain personal data and must be handled in accordance with data protection rules.

## Objections

**“Portfolios are more subjective than tests.”** The risk exists, and equivalent assessment is already a problem in Swedish schools. Co-assessment, clear assessment support and national tests as calibration are needed.

**“Pupils with support at home benefit.”** Portfolio work should take place at school under the teacher’s supervision.

**“It takes too much time.”** Portfolios require more time for assessment. The pilot should show how much, and whether the benefit justifies it.

## International experience

- **The New York Performance Standards Consortium** is a group of schools in New York that uses extensive pupil work instead of certain standardised tests.
