# Energy cooperatives

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Residents, farmers, companies and sometimes the municipality in an area jointly own a production facility, such as wind turbines, a solar park or a biogas plant, through an economic association. The members share in the returns and have influence over the project.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

When new production is built, those who live nearby bear a large part of the impact, while the benefit often ends up with owners elsewhere. Local ownership is a way to distribute the benefit so that more people in the area have reason to support a project. The new law on revenue sharing, which applies from July 2026, gives nearby residents the right to a share of the revenue from new wind power installations, but does not give ownership or influence.

## What already exists

- **Wind power cooperatives** have existed in Sweden since the 1990s, where members own shares in wind turbines.
- **Solar cell associations and tenant-owner associations** with shared solar cell installations.
- **Municipal energy companies**, which are a kind of local ownership through the municipality.
- **In Denmark**, local ownership of wind power has long been common, and the island of Samsø has become a well-known example of locally owned renewable energy.

## How it would work

- **Economic association** in which the members subscribe for shares.
- **Financing** through member contributions and bank loans, possibly with state loan guarantees.
- **Sale of electricity** on the electricity market, or, once the rules on energy sharing are in place, directly to the members.
- **Cooperation** with an experienced project developer or an energy company for technology, operation and the permit process.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Support for five to ten cooperative projects in different parts of the country over three to five years, in the form of advice and loan guarantees for early stages.

**Evaluation:** local acceptance before and after, how long the permit process takes compared with similar projects, members’ returns and how many participate.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost |
|------|---------|
| Advice and support for early stages | approx. SEK 2–3 million per year |
| Loan guarantees | depends on the size of the projects |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 1 million per year |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Permits:** Cooperative projects are assessed like other projects, including the municipal veto for wind power.
2. **Financial Supervisory Authority:** If shares are offered broadly to the public, rules on offers of securities may become applicable.
3. **Capital:** Wind power is capital-intensive. A cooperative may need to co-own with an energy company or the municipality.

## Objections

**“Cooperatives cannot carry large projects.”** Often not alone. Co-ownership, where the cooperative owns part of a larger project, can be a way.

**“It benefits those who have money to invest.”** Low contribution amounts and the possibility of instalment payments can broaden participation.

**“Local ownership does not guarantee acceptance.”** No. That should be evaluated.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Investigate whether the municipality or the municipal energy company can offer residents to own shares in new projects.

**If you live in a rural area:** Contact existing wind power or solar cell cooperatives to learn from their experiences.
