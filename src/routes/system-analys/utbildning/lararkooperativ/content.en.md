# Teacher cooperatives

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Teachers who together run a school as an economic association, where the teachers are members and govern the activities democratically. The school is an independent school, funded by school vouchers and subject to the same rules and supervision as other schools. Any surplus remains in the activities.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Teachers’ professional discretion and influence over their working situation affect both the working environment and teaching. At the same time, the debate about profits in schools is intense. A teacher cooperative is a way to combine greater influence for teachers with an activity in which surplus is not distributed to external owners.

## What already exists

- **Independent schools** can be run by limited companies, economic associations, non-profit associations and foundations. The Swedish Schools Inspectorate assesses applications to start an independent school.
- **Parent cooperatives** have long existed in preschool, and there are also staff cooperatives.
- **Idea-driven schools**, run by non-profit associations and foundations, exist in many places.

It is thus already possible for teachers to run a school as a cooperative. The proposal is about making it easier for more to do so.

## How it would work

- **Form of organisation:** an economic association in which the teachers at the school are members and make decisions about the direction of the activities.
- **Management:** the head teacher has the responsibility set out in the Education Act, and administration can be handled by employed staff or in cooperation with other cooperatives.
- **Financing:** school voucher, as for other independent schools.
- **Surplus:** reinvested in the activities.
- **Collective agreements:** the cooperative can sign collective agreements, and the teachers can be members of a union, for example Sweden’s Teachers.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Support for five to ten teacher groups that want to start or take over a school as a cooperative, over three years, with advice and the possibility of start-up loans.

**Evaluation:** pupils’ results and well-being, teachers’ working environment and staff turnover, and the schools’ finances.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost |
|------|---------|
| Advice and support | approx. SEK 2 million per year |
| Start-up loan (repaid) | approx. SEK 1–2 million per cooperative |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 1.5 million per year |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Licence:** A teacher cooperative applies for a licence from the Swedish Schools Inspectorate like other independent schools, with the same requirements for financial conditions and ownership and management assessment.
2. **Start-up capital:** Teacher groups often have less capital than established groups. Start-up loans or guarantees may be needed.
3. **Transfer of a municipal school:** If a municipal school is to continue as a cooperative, a decision by the municipality and a new licence are required.

## Objections

**“There will be more responsible authorities and more fragmentation.”** That is a relevant objection in a system where equivalence has already deteriorated. Cooperatives should therefore not be promoted at the expense of supervision and requirements.

**“Teachers want to teach, not run a business.”** Many do not. The proposal is aimed at those who want to, and administration can be shared with others.

**“It becomes an activity for attractive areas.”** Support can be directed particularly towards cooperatives in areas where it is difficult to recruit and retain teachers.

## What you can do

**If you are a teacher:** Investigate with colleagues whether there is interest, and contact the Swedish Schools Inspectorate and an adviser on cooperatives.

**If you are a municipal politician:** Find out how the municipality would view a school being run onwards by the teachers themselves.
