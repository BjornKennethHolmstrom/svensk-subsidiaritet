# Processing close to production

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Slaughter, dairy, mill and other processing on a smaller scale and close to the farms, through farm slaughter, mobile or cooperative units and clearer guidance for small actors. The aim is for small-scale producers to be able to process and sell their products without long transports to large facilities.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## What applies today

- **Farm slaughter:** Since a change in EU rules, animals can be killed on the farm where they are kept and the meat sold, if the slaughter takes place in cooperation with an approved slaughterhouse and the farm is suitable. The company De vilda in Järna was first in Sweden to be approved for this. The number of animals per occasion is limited.
- **Mobile slaughterhouses:** Mobile slaughterhouses are already used, among other things for reindeer slaughter, and a mobile unit can be approved together with a slaughterhouse.
- **Hygiene rules:** Food hygiene rules are mainly common within the EU, but allow some room for adaptation for small operations.

Earlier versions of this page claimed that Sweden has zero mobile slaughterhouses and that farm slaughter was only temporarily allowed during covid-19. That is not correct.

## Proposals

1. **Clearer guidance** from the Swedish Food Agency on how the hygiene rules can be applied in small and mobile facilities, for example mills, farm dairies and fermentation facilities.
2. **Support for shared facilities**, where several producers share a mill, a slaughterhouse or a processing premises, for example through investment support in agricultural policy.
3. **Coordinated inspection**, where the inspection authorities have expertise in small-scale processing and can provide decisions within a reasonable time.

**What we know:** Farm slaughter has been possible since a change in EU rules, and mobile slaughterhouses are already used, among other things for reindeer slaughter.

**Most important assumption:** That shared, smaller processing facilities make it possible for small-scale producers to process and sell profitably.

- **Stop if:** food safety fails in the facilities despite inspections.
- **Adjust if:** the facilities are used by few producers; then distances, fees or opening hours may need to be changed.
- **Continue if:** several producers use the facilities regularly, food safety is maintained and the operation can cover its costs.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three regional pilots, for example one with farm slaughter and mobile slaughter, one with a shared mill and one with shared processing premises for vegetables, over three years.

**Evaluation:** cost per unit, food safety, animal welfare, how many producers use the facility and whether the operation can cover its costs.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost |
|------|---------|
| Investment per facility | approx. SEK 0.5–2 million depending on type |
| Advice and coordination | approx. SEK 1 million per year |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million per year |

## Objections

**“Food safety cannot be guaranteed in small facilities.”** The same food safety requirements should apply. The question is how they can be met in a way that suits small operations.

**“It is more expensive per kilo.”** Yes, often. Processing close to production makes it possible to sell at a higher price, but does not suit everyone.

**“Animal welfare.”** Farm slaughter and mobile slaughter reduce transport, which can be better for the animals. This should be followed up.
