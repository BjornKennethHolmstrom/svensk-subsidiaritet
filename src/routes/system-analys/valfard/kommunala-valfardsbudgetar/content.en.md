# Municipal welfare budgets

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A pilot activity in which a number of municipalities receive fewer targeted central government grants and more general funds, as well as the opportunity to test working methods in social services and care other than those normally permitted by the regulations. National minimum levels, the right to appeal and independent evaluation apply throughout.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Municipal activities are financed mainly by municipal income tax and general central government grants, and the municipal economic equalisation system evens out differences in conditions between municipalities. In addition, municipalities receive a large number of targeted central government grants, often temporary and with their own conditions and reporting requirements. This makes it harder to plan long-term and to adapt interventions to local needs.

At the same time, needs vary greatly. A sparsely populated municipality with an ageing population and a metropolitan suburb with many young people have different conditions, but work within the same regulatory framework.

## How it would work

1. **Fewer targeted central government grants:** The targeted central government grants in selected areas, for example social services and elderly care, are replaced for the pilot municipalities by equivalent general funds.
2. **Exemptions to test new working methods:** Municipalities can apply to deviate from certain detailed regulations, if they show how the goals will be achieved and how individuals’ rights will be protected.
3. **National minimum levels:** The right to assistance, the national standard for social assistance, legal certainty and the possibility to appeal apply as usual.
4. **Evaluation and open reporting:** Each pilot municipality reports how the funds were used and what results were achieved. The results are compared with similar municipalities.
5. **Possibility to discontinue:** If a municipality does not meet the minimum levels, it returns to the ordinary rules.

The social insurance schemes, such as sickness benefit and parental benefit, are not included. They should remain national.

## Examples of what municipalities could test

| Conditions | Possible working method |
|-----------|----------------|
| Sparsely populated area with many older people | Coordinated home care, transport services and food distribution, with greater freedom of choice for the individual |
| Area with high unemployment among young people | Coordinated interventions from social services, the employment unit and associational life |
| Municipality with many long-term social assistance recipients | Longer decision periods and strengthened employment incentives |

**What we know:** A pilot activity with exemptions from the Social Services Act requires special legislation, and the proposal is therefore exploratory. Sweden has a precedent in the free municipality experiment in the 1980s.

**Most important assumption:** That fewer targeted central government grants and the opportunity to deviate from certain detailed regulations mean that municipalities adapt interventions better to local needs, without the most vulnerable being worse off.

**What we do not know:** How the free municipality experiment was evaluated and what it led to. This should be reviewed before the proposal is pursued further.

- **Stop if:** the outcomes for the most vulnerable groups, for example children in families receiving social assistance, deteriorate in the pilot municipalities compared with similar municipalities.
- **Adjust if:** the municipalities hardly use the opportunities to deviate from the rules; then detailed regulations may not be the biggest obstacle.
- **Continue if:** the outcomes are at least as good as in the comparison municipalities, and the municipalities can show examples of interventions that would not have been possible otherwise.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** 10–20 municipalities of different sizes and types over four to six years, following application.

**Evaluation:** independent researchers compare the pilot municipalities with similar municipalities without the pilot, with regard to, for example, child poverty, employment, quality of life for older people, homelessness and cost trends.

## Rough cost estimate

The redistribution of central government grants costs nothing extra in itself. Additional costs relate to evaluation and support for the pilot municipalities, proposed at SEK 20–30 million per year.

## Legal issues

1. **Legal basis for pilot schemes:** A pilot activity with exemptions from the Social Services Act requires special legislation. There is a Swedish precedent: the free municipality experiment in the 1980s, in which a number of municipalities and county councils were granted exemptions from central government regulations.
2. **Equivalence and legal certainty:** The exemptions must not cover the individual’s right to assistance or the possibility to appeal.
3. **Design of central government grants:** Which targeted grants can be converted into general funds needs to be investigated.

## Objections

**“Inequality between municipalities increases.”** That is a real risk. The minimum levels, the equalisation system and the possibility to discontinue the pilot are intended to counteract it. The evaluation should particularly monitor developments for the most vulnerable.

**“The state loses the ability to steer.”** Targeted central government grants are used to push through national priorities. The pilot tests whether goal-based steering and follow-up can replace some of that steering.

**“Municipalities lack capacity.”** Small municipalities may find it difficult to develop new working methods. Cooperation between municipalities and support from national authorities are needed.

## Experience to build on

- **The free municipality experiment** in Sweden in the 1980s and **frikommuneforsøket** in Norway during the same period.
- **The Trust Delegation** (2016–2019), which worked on trust-based steering in the public sector.
- **The municipal economic equalisation system**, which already redistributes resources according to needs and conditions.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal commissioner or municipal director:** Map which targeted central government grants the municipality receives and what the reporting costs in time and resources.

**If you are a member of the Riksdag:** Propose an inquiry into a new pilot activity, based on the experiences from the free municipality experiment.

**If you are a researcher:** Offer to design the evaluation of such a pilot.
