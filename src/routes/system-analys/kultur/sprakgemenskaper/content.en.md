# Language communities

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Support for cultural creation in languages other than Swedish, such as literature, music, performing arts and podcasts, where advisory groups with knowledge of the respective language and cultural life participate in the assessment. The proposal complements the support that already exists for the national minority languages.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

A large number of languages are spoken in Sweden. Cultural creators who write, sing or perform theatre in languages other than Swedish can find it difficult to obtain support, partly because it can be hard for assessors to evaluate works in languages they do not understand.

## What already exists

- **National minority languages:** Sami, Finnish, Meänkieli, Romani Chib and Yiddish have special protection under the Act on National Minorities and Minority Languages. There is state support for culture and literature in these languages, and the Sami Parliament distributes state funds for Sami culture.
- **Libraries:** Under the Libraries Act, public libraries must pay particular attention to the national minorities and to people with a mother tongue other than Swedish, among other things by offering literature in their languages.
- **Mother tongue instruction** is offered in schools to pupils with a mother tongue other than Swedish.

Earlier versions of this page claimed that cultural support is only given to expressions in Swedish. That is not correct.

## How it would work

1. **A pot for cultural creation in other languages**, for example at the Swedish Arts Council or a region.
2. **Application in one’s own language**, with a short summary in Swedish.
3. **Advisory groups** with people who have knowledge of the language and its cultural life in Sweden, for example writers, translators, researchers and association representatives. The groups provide a basis, but the decision is made by the authority that administers the support.
4. **Support for translation**, both from and into Swedish, so that works can reach more people.

Support is distributed according to quality and feasibility, not according to how many people speak a particular language.

## Why not language councils with their own decision-making power

An earlier proposal on this page was that each large language group should have its own council, elected by the speakers, with its own budget distributed according to the number of speakers. That has several problems:

- **There is no language statistics.** Sweden does not register which languages the population speaks, so it is not possible to distribute money according to the number of speakers.
- **Registers of language and origin are sensitive.** Registering people by language so that they can vote in a council would involve processing data that can reveal ethnic origin, which is particularly protected personal data.
- **Language communities are not uniform.** The same language is spoken by people with different origins, religions and political views. An elected council risks being dominated by certain groups.

Therefore, advisory groups and a decision by an authority are proposed here, instead of independent councils.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Support of, for example, SEK 10–20 million per year for three years, for example at the Swedish Arts Council or in one or two regions.

**Evaluation:** number of applications and in which languages, what is produced, how the works reach an audience, and whether translation support leads to works reaching new readers and listeners.

## Objections

**“It makes integration more difficult.”** Cultural creation in other languages can instead strengthen contact between groups, especially if it is combined with translation and joint events.

**“Swedish-speaking cultural creators are disadvantaged.”** The support is a smaller pot that complements other cultural support.

**“Strong groups take over.”** Advisory groups with a broad composition and decisions by an authority should reduce that risk.

## What you can do

**If you work at a library:** Investigate which languages are spoken in the library’s area and whether there are local authors or musicians who could take part in events.

**If you are a cultural creator in a language other than Swedish:** Contact a study association or the region’s cultural administration about opportunities for support.
