# Health centres close to everyday life

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Primary care, preventive work and municipal services are co-located in premises that people already visit, such as libraries, citizen offices or family centres. The teams can consist of district nurses, physiotherapists, dietitians, counsellors and health guides, with access to doctors on site or digitally.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

The transformation towards good and close care aims to make primary care the hub of healthcare, with more continuity and more care close to the patient. In many places, health centres, social services, libraries and associational life are in different locations without contact with one another. For people with multiple needs, or for those who hesitate to seek care, a single place with a low threshold can make a difference.

## What already exists

- **Family centres**, where maternity care, child health care, open preschool and social services are under the same roof.
- **Mobile teams and digital care meetings** as a way to provide care in sparsely populated areas.
- **Team-based working methods** in primary care, where different professions share patient contacts.

## How it would work

- **Co-location:** parts of primary care are in the same premises as, for example, a library, citizen office or family centre.
- **Teamwork:** different professions share the tasks, and the doctor has medical responsibility.
- **Open activities:** for example lectures, walking groups and parent meetings in cooperation with study associations and associations.
- **Adaptation to the location:** in sparsely populated areas, a health centre can be a combination of a smaller premises, mobile teams and digital care meetings.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three pilots over four years: one in a city district, one in a residential area with poorer health and one in a sparsely populated area. The locations are chosen in consultation with regions and municipalities.

**Evaluation:** accessibility and continuity, patients’ experience, who is reached, emergency visits and admissions compared with comparable health centres, staff working environment and cost per patient.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost per pilot |
|------|-------------------|
| Adaptation of premises | approx. SEK 1–3 million, one-off cost |
| Additional staff, for example health guides and counsellors | approx. SEK 1–2 million per year |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Responsible authority:** Primary care is the region’s responsibility, while libraries, social services and leisure are municipal. Co-location requires agreements on premises, costs and responsibility.
2. **Confidentiality:** The confidentiality rules of healthcare and social services also apply in shared premises. Exchange of information requires consent or legal support.
3. **Remuneration models:** The regions’ remuneration to health centres affects which working methods pay off. Remuneration may need to reward continuity and preventive work, not only the number of visits.
4. **Patient safety and working environment:** Premises for care must meet requirements for hygiene, safety and confidentiality.

## Objections

**“It will become more expensive.”** Co-location may cost more at the beginning. If it reduces costs over time, the evaluation should show this.

**“Patients want to see a doctor.”** Those who need a doctor should get one. Teamwork should free up doctor time, not replace it.

**“Confidentiality becomes harder to maintain.”** The premises need to be designed so that conversations and medical records are protected.

## International experience

- **The Nuka model** in Alaska, where the indigenous population itself governs its primary care, has been reported to reduce emergency visits and hospital care.
- **Buurtzorg** in the Netherlands, where small self-governing nurse groups provide home care, has attracted international attention.

Both operate in systems other than the Swedish one, and the experiences cannot be transferred directly.

## What you can do

**If you are a regional politician:** Visit a family centre and ask what would be required for the same working method to cover more age groups.

**If you are a municipal civil servant:** Take stock of the municipality’s premises that could be shared with primary care, and invite the region to a discussion.
