# Cultural heritage in school

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Working methods in which pupils both learn about a shared Swedish cultural heritage and explore traditions, stories and history in their own family and local environment, and compare them. The proposal sees the shared and the local as complements, not opposites.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Background: the canon debate

In September 2025, a state committee presented the report *A Cultural Canon for Sweden* (SOU 2025:92), with 100 works and reference points that have shaped Swedish culture and history. The purpose, according to the terms of reference, was to make Swedish culture accessible to more people and provide shared frames of reference, as a tool for education, community and inclusion.

The proposal has met with both support and criticism. Supporters argue that shared references are important in a society with great diversity. Critics, including representatives of the national minorities, argue that their history and culture were given too little space in both the process and the result.

The question of what should be counted as part of the shared cultural heritage is thus politically alive. This page does not take a position for or against a canon, but proposes a working method in school that can be used regardless.

## What schools already do

The curricula already contain wording about cultural heritage, and about the culture, language, religion and history of the national minorities. Pupils with a mother tongue other than Swedish have the right to mother tongue instruction. Earlier versions of this page claimed that the curriculum prescribes a fixed list of authors and epochs. That is not correct: the syllabuses state core content but not which works must be read.

## How it would work

- **Two perspectives in the same work:** pupils work with works and events counted as part of the shared Swedish cultural heritage, and interview family members or older people in the local environment about traditions, stories and memories.
- **Comparisons:** pupils compare, for example, how holidays are celebrated, how stories are passed on or how people have moved, within Sweden and to Sweden.
- **Source criticism:** oral accounts and family memories are tested against other sources, such as archives and historical literature.
- **Presentation:** the results are presented in a portfolio or exhibition, preferably at the library.

The work applies to all pupils, regardless of background. A pupil whose family has lived in the same place for generations has just as much to discover as a pupil whose parents come from another country.

**What we know:** A state committee presented in 2025 a proposal for a Swedish cultural canon (SOU 2025:92). The proposal has been criticised, among others by representatives of the national minorities.

**Most important assumption:** That pupils who explore both a shared cultural heritage and traditions in their own local environment learn more and experience their background being taken seriously.

- **Stop if:** families in the follow-up describe the work with interviews as intrusive, and this cannot be corrected with other sources.
- **Adjust if:** pupils engage but their knowledge in history and Swedish does not change.
- **Continue if:** pupils’ engagement and knowledge are at least as good as in comparable classes, and both pupils with Swedish and foreign backgrounds describe the work as meaningful.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Ten schools in different municipalities over three years, in cooperation with libraries, museums and local heritage associations.

**Evaluation:** pupils’ engagement and knowledge in history and Swedish, their experience of their background being taken seriously, and teachers’ experiences.

## Objections

**“Swedish pupils should learn Swedish history.”** The proposal does not remove any of the shared content. It adds a way of working with it.

**“It is identity politics.”** The working method starts from all pupils’ experiences, not from certain groups’. The shared cultural heritage is part of the work, not something set against it.

**“Families do not want to share.”** Participation should be voluntary, and pupils should be able to choose sources other than their own family, for example a tradition in the local environment.

**“Teachers do not have time.”** The work should replace other tasks, not be added on. The pilots should show what is required.

## Experience to build on

- **The local heritage movement and local archives**, with long experience of documenting local history.
- **The Multicultural Centre in Botkyrka**, which works with cultural heritage and migration.
- **Oral history** as a method in history teaching.

## What you can do

**If you are a teacher:** Try letting pupils interview an older person about a tradition and compare it with a tradition they have read about.

**If you are a head teacher:** Contact a library, local heritage association or museum about cooperation.
