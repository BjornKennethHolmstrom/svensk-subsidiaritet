# Learning Rings

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Recurring projects in which a group of pupils works for a term together with a few professionals or other knowledgeable people from the local community, such as a carpenter, a programmer, a grower or a local historian. The teacher leads the work and links it to the goals of the syllabuses.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Many pupils rarely encounter the knowledge that exists in working life and associational life outside school, especially in higher education preparatory programmes. At the same time, in most places there are people with knowledge that pupils would benefit from, and many who would gladly share it. Practical projects can also make theoretical subjects more comprehensible, for example mathematics by building something or Swedish by making a podcast.

## What already exists

- **Workplace-based learning (APL)** and apprenticeship training in upper secondary vocational programmes.
- **Skapande skola**, a state grant that enables schools to cooperate with artists and cultural creators.
- **The Cultural Schoolbag** in Norway, where artists and cultural practitioners regularly work with schools.
- **Guest lectures and study visits**, which most schools already use but often as single elements.

## How it would work

- **A group of 10–20 pupils** and two to five knowledgeable people from the local community.
- **Recurring meetings** during a term, for example 8–12 occasions, at the school or at a workplace.
- **A joint project**, for example building something, starting a pupil company, documenting local history or growing.
- **The teacher** plans, leads and links the project to the goals of the syllabus, and is responsible for assessment.
- **The pupils document** their work, which can be used as a basis for the teacher’s assessment.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Around fifty Learning Rings in 10–20 municipalities of different types over three years.

**Evaluation:** pupils’ motivation and attendance, their knowledge in the subjects to which the projects are linked compared with a comparison group, teachers’ workload and how cooperation with the local community develops.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Per Learning Ring and term |
|------|-----------------------------|
| Fee to knowledgeable people | approx. SEK 10,000 |
| Materials | approx. SEK 3,000 |
| Teacher’s planning time | approx. SEK 5,000 |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Criminal record check:** Under law, those who work in school with children must provide an extract from the criminal record. This should also apply to knowledgeable people who participate regularly.
2. **Responsibility for teaching:** The teacher always has responsibility for teaching and grading. The knowledgeable people contribute, but do not assess.
3. **Teaching time:** If a Learning Ring is to replace part of the teaching in a subject, it must be clearly linked to the subject’s syllabus.
4. **Insurance:** Participating knowledgeable people and pupils need to be covered by insurance, especially when working at workplaces.

## Objections

**“Teaching is being shifted onto amateurs.”** The teacher always leads the work. The knowledgeable people contribute practical knowledge, as guest lecturers do today, but more regularly.

**“Municipalities do not have resources.”** The costs are limited, but coordination requires time. The pilot should show how much.

**“It only reaches certain pupils.”** The Learning Rings should be free of charge and open to all pupils in the group.

## What you can do

**If you are a teacher or head teacher:** Invite a professional to a recurring project over a few weeks, and link it to the goals of the syllabus.

**If you are professionally active or a pensioner:** Contact a nearby school and tell them what you can contribute.
