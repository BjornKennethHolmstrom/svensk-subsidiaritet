# GAE as an Analytical Lens

## A tool, not a synonym for evidence

Governance as Engineering (GAE) is a series of 29 published reports developing formal and conceptual models of governance: latency, signal fidelity, requisite variety, coordination tax, adaptation bottlenecks, boundary dynamics, and more.

All GAE reports are already fully published at [bjornkennethholmstrom.org](https://www.bjornkennethholmstrom.org/), and the associated simulations live on [GitHub](https://github.com/BjornKennethHolmstrom/gae-governance-simulator). We don't duplicate that content here. This page — and the GAE references attached to research topics, cases, measures and hypotheses — is instead the **navigational layer** between our empirical question and GAE's technical depth.

---

## Why the separation matters

GAE is **one of our analytical tools — not synonymous with the evidence itself.**

```text
                   SVENSK SUBSIDIARITET
                           │
          ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
          │                                 │
     Governance                        Evidence
    Architecture                         │
          │                      ┌─────────┴─────────┐
          │                      │                   │
     What should                 Research         Models
     the system                  Cases            & simulations
     look like?                  Data
          │                      Synthesis
          │
          └──────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                         │
                    Architecture
                     iteration
```

A GAE result is, as explained under **[How We Know](/evidens/sa-vet-vi/)**, often an **[R]** result: rigorous within its formal model. That doesn't automatically make it a claim about real institutions. We therefore always mark whether a given GAE concept is being used as [R] (rigorous model result), [IP] (disciplined institutional interpretation), or [H] (heuristic analogy) in a given context.

## What a "dive deeper" connection looks like in practice

When a hypothesis or case references GAE, we always give two separate links:

> **Read the full GAE report →** (theoretical derivation, formal model)
> **View the simulation / code →** (reproducible implementation on GitHub)

The technical source material remains authoritative for the detailed derivation and implementation. The Evidence page only explains **why** the work is relevant to the empirical research question.

That lets a reader follow the full chain:

> empirical study → our case analysis → relevant GAE mechanism → model → simulation/code.
