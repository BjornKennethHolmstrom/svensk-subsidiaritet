# Early local decisions

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** In their energy planning, municipalities produce early and reasoned decisions on where new electricity production, such as wind power and solar parks, can be built, in dialogue with residents, businesses, Sami villages and other affected parties. The decisions are combined with local benefit, through revenue sharing, local ownership or other commitments.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Municipalities already have great influence over new wind power through the municipal veto in the Environmental Code. A mapping commissioned by the wind power industry shows that 64 percent of the projects that were relevant in 2020–2024 were stopped by the veto. Criticism of the veto has included that it lacks decision criteria, requirements for justification and time limits, which makes decisions difficult to predict. Both developers and residents have an interest in early and clear decisions.

## A changed direction

Earlier versions of this page proposed that municipal energy councils should have veto rights against large energy projects and that third-party appeals should be limited. That was based on the misconception that municipalities lack influence. They already have a veto for wind power, which is used often. Limiting the right of residents and organisations to appeal would also be an intrusion into legal certainty. The proposal has therefore been replaced by one that focuses on early decisions and shared benefit.

## How it would work

1. **Energy planning:** In its energy plan and comprehensive plan, the municipality identifies areas where new production can be tested, and areas where it is not suitable.
2. **Dialogue:** Residents, Sami villages, businesses and environmental organisations are invited in early, before individual projects are planned.
3. **Early decisions:** When a developer asks, the municipality gives an early and reasoned decision on the location.
4. **Local benefit:** The municipality negotiates local ownership, local community funds or other commitments, in addition to the statutory revenue sharing for nearby residents.

**What we know:** A mapping commissioned by Swedish Wind Energy shows that 64 percent of wind power projects in 2020–2024 were stopped by the municipal veto.

**Most important assumption:** That early, reasoned decisions and shared benefit change how municipalities view new production.

- **Stop if:** the municipalities in the trial say no as often as other municipalities, despite early decisions and local benefit; that argues that the decision needs to be moved upwards, for example by changing the municipal veto.
- **Adjust if:** decisions are given early but are barely justified; then clearer requirements for justification are needed.
- **Continue if:** the proportion of approved projects is higher than in comparable municipalities, and Sami villages and other affected parties experience the process as better.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Five municipalities in different parts of the country over three years.

**Evaluation:** how long it takes to get a decision, the proportion of projects that receive approval, local acceptance, and how Sami villages and other affected parties experience the process.

## Legal issues

1. **The municipal veto:** The inquiry *A legally secure wind power review* (SOU 2021:53) has proposed changes to make the veto more predictable. The proposals should be followed up.
2. **Sami rights:** Sami villages and other Sami representatives need to be able to participate in the process in a way that takes account of the conditions for reindeer herding.

## Objections

**“Municipalities will say no anyway.”** Possibly, but early decisions save time and money for everyone, and shared benefit can change the calculation for the municipality.

**“The state should remove the veto.”** That is a possible path, which has been investigated. It would move the decision upwards, which may be justified for a national issue. The trade-off between local acceptance and national needs is political.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Review whether the municipality’s energy plan identifies where new production can be built, and how the municipality has used the veto.
