# Transition dynamics

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** How changes in the food system can be tested step by step: through many small experiments that are allowed to fail, a breadth of different paths at the same time, and systematic learning about what works.

## Small experiments that are allowed to fail

In complex systems, it is difficult to know in advance what will work. It is therefore wise to test many smaller experiments, where a failure does not threaten the whole, instead of one large reform. This requires that municipalities, authorities and organisations dare to report also what did not work, and that evaluation is planned from the start.

## Several paths at the same time

Change can be driven by different actors in parallel:

- **Municipalities**, through the design of procurement, land use and planning.
- **Producers**, through cooperatives, shared facilities and direct sales.
- **Civil society**, through REKO rings, growing networks and consumer cooperatives.
- **The state**, through preparedness, regulations and agricultural policy.

If one path stalls, the others can continue.

## From local to national

Solutions that work locally can spread to more places, and in some cases form the basis for changed rules. One example is farm slaughter: there was long-standing demand from farmers, and when the EU rules changed, the first Swedish farms could be approved. Research on complex systems speaks of such processes as changes moving between different levels of a system.

## Crises as opportunities

Crises can create an opportunity to test new solutions, but also a temptation to simply restore the old. Exercises in which municipalities, farmers and authorities go through what happens in the event of, for example, an interruption in the import of fuel or fertiliser can show where the vulnerabilities are, before a real crisis occurs.

## Not locking in too early

National rules and standards should preferably build on experiences from trials. Temporary exemptions and pilot activities can be given an end date, which can be extended if they work.

## Objections

**“What is needed is decisiveness, not experiments.”** Trials are a way of acting quickly without committing to a solution that may not work.

**“Who coordinates?”** Common ways of evaluating and sharing experiences are needed, for example through county administrative boards, the Swedish Board of Agriculture and researchers.
