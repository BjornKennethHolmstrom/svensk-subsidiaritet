# Social prescribing

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Primary care refers patients whose problems are largely connected to loneliness, inactivity or lack of context to activities in associations, study associations and other local activities. A coordinator, often called a link worker, meets the patient, helps find something that suits them and follows up. The proposal builds on physical activity on prescription and culture on prescription, which already exist in Sweden.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Some of those who seek primary care for tiredness, low mood or anxiety also live with loneliness or lack a sense of context. Treatment in healthcare helps many, but does not always affect the social circumstances. At the same time, there is a large range of associations and activities that healthcare rarely has contact with.

## What already exists

- **Physical activity on prescription (FaR)** has long been used in Swedish healthcare, with a written prescription and follow-up.
- **Culture on prescription** has been tested in several regions.
- **Nature-based rehabilitation**, for example the rehabilitation garden in Alnarp, has been used for people with stress-related ill-health.
- **Social prescribing** has been expanded in British primary care, with link workers at many health centres.

## How it would work

1. A doctor, nurse, counsellor or physiotherapist assesses that social factors play a major role in the patient’s problems.
2. The patient is offered, as a complement to or in some cases instead of other treatment, contact with a link worker.
3. The link worker meets the patient, goes through what might suit them and helps with the first contact.
4. The patient participates for a period, for example 8–12 weeks.
5. The link worker follows up, using simple rating scales for well-being.

Social prescribing does not replace treatment for moderate or severe mental ill-health. The assessment of what is suitable is made by healthcare.

### What we know

- Physical activity on prescription has long been used in Swedish healthcare, and culture on prescription has been tested in several regions.
- A systematic review of British evaluations (Bickerdike et al. 2017) found that social prescribing is advocated and introduced on a large scale, but that the evidence was insufficient to assess either effect or cost-effectiveness. Of 15 evaluations, only one was randomised, and the risk of bias was high.

### What we assume

- That some of primary care’s patients have problems that are largely connected to loneliness and lack of context, and that activities in associational life can affect them.
- That a link worker is needed for the referral to lead to actual participation, and that a written referral is not enough.
- That associational life has the capacity to receive the participants.

### What we do not know

- **How the whole behaves.** The combination of healthcare’s authority, a link worker and the community of associational life can produce a participation that none of the parts provides alone. It can also fail in a way that the parts do not predict: if the referral is perceived as healthcare dismissing the patient’s problems as “just social”, confidence in healthcare can decrease.
- Whether the effects, if any exist, remain after the period with the link worker is over.
- Which patients benefit from it, and which do not.

### How we would test it

- **Test:** around ten health centres over three years, with comparison against similar health centres without link workers, and if possible with random allocation of which patients are offered a link worker.
- **Stop if:** patients in the follow-up to a significant extent experience the referral as their problems not being taken seriously; or if patients who need treatment are instead referred to activities.
- **Adjust if:** few of those referred start participating in an activity; or if the associations cannot receive more participants.
- **Continue if:** participants report better well-being and less loneliness than the comparison group after six and twelve months, and staff assess that the working method is worth the time.

The threshold values are to be determined before the pilot starts and not changed afterwards.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Around ten health centres in different types of areas, over three years, with a link worker at half-time to full-time per health centre.

**Evaluation:** compare patients who received social prescribing with similar patients who did not, with regard to well-being, loneliness, healthcare visits, sick leave and medication use. The evaluation should also examine how the associations experience it.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost per health centre and year |
|------|-----------------------------|
| Link worker (0.5–1.0 FTE) | approx. SEK 0.3–0.6 million |
| Fees for activities and remuneration to associations | approx. SEK 0.1 million |
| Training and evaluation | approx. SEK 0.1 million |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Documentation:** Social prescribing may need its own intervention code to be followed up, in the same way as FaR.
2. **Transfer of information:** If the link worker is employed by the municipality or an association, the patient’s consent is needed to share information.
3. **Cooperation:** Primary care is the region’s responsibility, while many activities are run by associations with municipal support. Cooperation between region and municipality is needed.

## Objections

**“The evidence is weak.”** Yes. A systematic review of the British evaluations found that the evidence is insufficient to assess the effect. This is a reason to test with careful evaluation, not to assume that it works.

**“It medicalises everyday life.”** Referring to a choir via healthcare can make loneliness a medical problem. At the same time, healthcare may be the only contact an isolated person has.

**“The associations cannot manage.”** That is why the link worker is needed, and a small remuneration to associations that receive participants.

## What you can do

**If you work in healthcare:** Find out which activities exist in the local area, and whether your region has FaR or culture on prescription.

**If you are a regional politician:** Propose a pilot with link workers and evaluation, in cooperation with the municipalities and associational life.

**If you are active in an association:** Contact the local health centre and offer to receive participants.
