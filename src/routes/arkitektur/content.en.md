# Governance Architecture

## Finding the right shape for governance in a complex society

The Swedish model rests on a clear central chain: voters → Parliament → Government → administration. It has worked well, but it rests on an assumption that is rarely stated out loud — that one and the same political majority should have the right answer to every question, whether it concerns climate, defence, taxation or culture.

The question we explore here is not whether Sweden should have "more" or "less" central government. It is:

> **Which allocation of decisions, knowledge, responsibility and coordination works best for which kind of problem?**

That is an engineering question rather than an ideological one, and it can be examined systematically. This is what we call **Governance as Engineering**.

---

## Five building blocks

This section collects the analytical tools we use to explore the answer.

### [The Governance Spectrum →](/arkitektur/spektrum/)

Twelve governance architectures, ordered from today's Swedish parliamentarism toward a fully polycentric, GGF-like form of governance. The spectrum shows that "more decentralized" is not a single dimension, but several that can move independently of each other.

### [The Architecture Matrix →](/arkitektur/matris/)

A comparison of the twelve architectures across ten properties — democratic legitimacy, coordination, accountability, resilience, and more. The matrix shows where each architecture gains something, and where it risks losing something else along the way.

### [Adaptive Subsidiarity →](/arkitektur/adaptiv-subsidiaritet/)

Subsidiarity does not mean "as local as possible." It means decisions should be made at the level that can actually handle them best — and that level can change as we learn more.

### [Polycentric Governance →](/arkitektur/polycentrisk-styrning/)

What happens when we stop assuming there must be one governance architecture for the entire state, and instead let different policy domains have partly autonomous, coordinating governance ecosystems.

### [Meta-Governance →](/arkitektur/meta-styrning/)

The final, and perhaps most important, building block: the capacity to continuously ask whether the current allocation of power, knowledge and responsibility is still correctly designed — and to change it when it isn't.

---

## How they fit together

![Platform system architecture](/images/architecture/website-structure.en.svg)

The spectrum describes **where** we can move. The matrix describes **what we gain and lose** along the way. Adaptive subsidiarity and polycentric governance describe **the principles** that make a more distributed architecture meaningful rather than chaotic. Meta-governance describes **how the system learns** to correct itself.

Together, these do not form a finished blueprint for Sweden. They are a *design language* — a way of asking better questions about governance, which we can then test against real Swedish problems in our policy laboratories.
