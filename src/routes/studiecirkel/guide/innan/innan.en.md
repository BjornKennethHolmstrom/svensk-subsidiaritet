# **Before you begin – Hosting guide**

**Welcome.**
You are holding the material for a study circle about how society's systems work in everyday life, and about where decisions should be made. The circle is not about party politics, but it has a starting point: the principle of subsidiarity, that decisions should be made as close as possible to those affected, but higher up when necessary. Participants should get to test that principle, not just adopt it.

## Your role
You are not a teacher. You are a **gardener**.
Your task is not to have the answers, but to make sure all voices can grow, including those that are sceptical of the material's starting point.

## The circle's structure (4 meetings)
1.  **The experiences:** How do we encounter society's systems, and when do they work well or badly?
2.  **The compass:** Examining where decisions are made, whose knowledge is used and how robust a system is.
3.  **The principle:** What does subsidiarity mean, and what are the arguments for and against?
4.  **The action:** Do we want to do something together, and if so, what?

## Golden rules
* **Systems, not people:** If someone complains about "politician X", redirect to "what incentives steer politician X?"
* **Around the circle:** Make sure the quiet ones get space.
* **Coffee:** The most important technology for Swedish collaboration. Fika is mandatory.

## **How to invite people (without sounding like a cult)**

**Text/email template:**
> "Hi [Name]! I'm gathering a few neighbours/colleagues to talk about how we can make our everyday life better – not by complaining, but by thinking in new ways about how society works. There will be fika, good company and no requirement to 'believe in' anything in particular. Interested in giving it a try? 📅 [Date] at [Time] at [Place]. Get in touch if you want to join!"

**What to answer to common questions:**
- *"Is this politics?"* – "It's not party politics, but it is about how society is governed. The material is based on an idea that decisions should be made close by, and together we test when that holds and when it doesn't."
- *"What does it cost?"* – "Nothing, except that you might bring a little fika."
- *"I don't know anything about this..."* – "Perfect! Then you have no preconceptions."

## **Room preparations (the Swedish magic)**

1. **Chairs in a circle** – no head of the table, no back row
2. **Fika station** – coffee, tea, something to nibble on (shared responsibility works well)
3. **Tools** – flipchart/paper, pens, slips of paper, and **a quiet signal** (could be a bell or just "raising a hand")
4. **The temperature** – Swedes do well at 20–22°C, not too warm

## **Your first five minutes (exactly what you say)**

> "Welcome, everyone. Thank you for coming.
>
> I've invited you here because I believe we need to start talking in a new way about what isn't working in our everyday lives. Not by complaining about people or parties, but by looking at *the systems* – how decision-making works, where knowledge exists, and how we can become more self-sufficient right where we live.
>
> I don't have any answers. But I have some good questions that I think can lead somewhere new. And I have coffee.
>
> We will meet four times. Today we start by simply noting what feels off. Next time we learn a tool for analysing. The third time we look at solutions. The fourth time we decide on something small that we can actually do.
>
> The only thing required of you is that you listen and share what you feel comfortable sharing. No one will get homework or be interrogated.
>
> Shall we start with everyone saying their name and one word about how it feels to be here tonight?"
