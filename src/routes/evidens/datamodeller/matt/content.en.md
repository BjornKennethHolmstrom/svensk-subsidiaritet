# Measures

## Making abstract governance concepts measurable

These are the project's own operationalizations of concepts that would otherwise stay abstract. **None of these are established, validated indices yet** — each is a specification for future data collection, clearly marked with its assumptions and limitations, in line with GAE's own caution that some indices and thresholds are heuristic rather than derived.

---

## Built so far

### [Decision Proximity →](/evidens/datamodeller/matt/beslutsnarhet/)
The actual distance between a decision and the people affected by it — as distinct from jurisdiction size or formal responsibility. The corpus's single highest-priority measurement gap.

### [Authority Depth →](/evidens/datamodeller/matt/auktoritetsdjup/)
How far effective decision-making authority actually travels below a formal administrative boundary. The measure the "nested centralization" hypothesis (H4) needs to become comparable across cases.

---

## Planned

The following measures are named and scoped in the project's content specification but not yet built out as individual pages:

- **Information Distance** — how far relevant local information travels, and how much is lost, before reaching a decision-maker.
- **Coordination Burden** — the ongoing cost of maintaining shared understanding and joint action across multiple actors sharing a function.
- **Functional Scale Fit** — how well a governance boundary matches the actual functional or geographic extent of the problem it addresses.
- **Capacity** — administrative, financial and analytical ability to plan, implement and evaluate policy at a given level.
- **Accountability Distance** — how difficult it is to trace a specific outcome back to a specific responsible actor.
- **Adaptation Latency** — how long it takes a governance system to detect a mismatch between its design and outcomes, and to correct it.

Each will get its own page, following the same template as Decision Proximity and Authority Depth, once there's a concrete first attempt to operationalize it against real data — we'd rather under-promise here than list eight "measures" that are really just names.
