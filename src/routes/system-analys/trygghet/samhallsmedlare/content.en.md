# Community mediators

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** People with strong roots in an area, such as coaches, association leaders, parents or people who have themselves left crime behind, are recruited, trained and paid to catch and mediate conflicts before they lead to violence. The mediators work in cooperation with social services and the police, but are not part of the police.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Conflicts between young people, between families or between groups can escalate quickly. Those who first see the signs are rarely the police, but people who are present in everyday life: coaches, field assistants, parents, association people. Some of them already function today as informal mediators, but without training, support or a mandate.

The new Social Services Act, in force since July 2025, places greater emphasis on preventive work and gives social services the opportunity to offer certain interventions without an individual needs assessment. This creates space for this kind of outreach and preventive work within existing law.

## How it would work

A community mediator:

- has the trust of different groups in the area and knows its networks and codes
- is trained in conflict mediation, conversations with young people, de-escalation and the rules that apply to children, confidentiality and the duty to report
- works part-time or full-time moving around the area, listening and catching conflicts early
- invites parties to talks and helps them reach an agreement that both can accept
- hands over to the police and social services when there are threats of serious violence, weapons or crimes against children

The mediator does not replace the police and has no powers beyond those of others. The authority comes from the person’s trust in the area.

**Training (proposal: 200 hours, including supervised practice):**

| Module | Content | Hours |
|-------|----------|--------|
| The dynamics of conflict | How conflicts escalate and can be broken | 20 |
| Mediation technique | Active listening, reframing, agreements | 40 |
| Children and young people | Children’s rights, duty to report, confidentiality | 20 |
| De-escalation and personal safety | Threatening situations, when to withdraw | 20 |
| Norms and context | Honour-related issues, family structures, religious aspects | 20 |
| Boundaries and cooperation | When the police and social services should be brought in | 20 |
| Supervised practice | Field work together with an experienced mediator | 60 |

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Around 100 mediators in 20–30 municipalities over three years, in areas where the municipality’s situational picture shows a need.

**Recruitment:** The municipality or a non-profit organisation recruits. Candidates must have roots in the area and references from associations and other local actors. Previous convictions need not disqualify, but every recruitment requires careful background checks, a probationary employment period and close supervision.

**Organisation:**
- employment with the municipality, a non-profit organisation or a cooperative
- a supervisor, for example a social worker or psychologist, for regular reflection
- a coordinating function in the municipality that receives cases, quality-assures them and handles difficult cases
- clear routines for cooperation with social services and the local police

**Evaluation:** number of cases, proportion of agreements that hold after three and six months, change in violence and perceived safety compared with similar areas without mediators, and how young people, parents and other actors experience the initiative. The proposal is that a research group follows the pilot from the start.

## Legal issues to investigate

1. **Legal basis:** It should be investigated whether the new Social Services Act’s provisions on preventive work and interventions without needs assessment are sufficient for the municipality to employ or engage mediators, or whether clarification is needed.
2. **Confidentiality and duty of secrecy:** A mediator employed by the municipality within social services is covered by social services confidentiality. For mediators with non-profit organisations, the question of confidentiality needs to be resolved. The duty to report when children are harmed must apply regardless of who runs the service.
3. **Status of the agreements:** Mediated agreements are voluntary and not legally binding. This should be made clear to the parties.

## Objections

**“Former criminals may continue their activities.”** The risk is real and is the most difficult question in the proposal. In areas where criminal actors have influence, they may try to influence or place their own people. Recruitment, supervision, transparency and cooperation with the police are therefore central.

**“The police are bypassed.”** The mediators handle conflicts that are not yet crimes, or that the parties can resolve themselves. Serious crimes must always go to the police.

**“200 hours is not enough.”** The mediators are not to investigate or decide on interventions. But the training needs to be tested, and the requirements may need to be raised.

**“The mediator may themselves be subjected to violence.”** Yes. Mediators should not work alone in high-risk situations, should have alarms and support, and must be able to withdraw without that counting as a failure.

## Experience to build on

- **Field assistants** exist in many municipalities and do similar outreach work, but often without a clear mandate to mediate.
- **Mediation in connection with crime:** municipalities must offer mediation to young offenders, and the Act on Mediation in Connection with Crime regulates how it is carried out. That is mediation after a crime; community mediators would work before.
- **Sluta skjut in Malmö** is based on Group Violence Intervention, where the police, the Prison and Probation Service, the municipality and civil society together direct a message to groups that drive violence and offer support to those who want to leave. It is not a mediation model, but it shows how civil society can have a role in coordinated work.
- **Cure Violence** is an international model in which so-called interrupters, often with their own experience of crime, work to prevent retaliatory violence. The evaluations show mixed results, which argues for testing cautiously and evaluating carefully.
- **The Norwegian Mediation Service (Konfliktrådet)** is a state mediation service with local offices that, among other things, handles mediation in connection with crime and penalties for young people.

## What you can do

**If you work in social services or are a municipal politician:** Find out which people in an area already function as informal mediators, and investigate whether they want training and a clearer mandate in a small pilot.

**If you are an association leader:** Help find candidates and provide references.

**If you are a researcher:** Offer to evaluate a pilot from the start, preferably with comparison areas.
