# Requisite Variety (GAE IV)

**Full report:** [Paper IV: Requisite Variety and the Commons](https://www.bjornkennethholmstrom.org/working-papers/requisite-variety-and-the-commons)
**Simulation / code:** [gae-governance-simulator](https://github.com/BjornKennethHolmstrom/gae-governance-simulator)

---

## What it's about

Requisite variety, in Ashby's original formulation, says a control system needs at least as much internal variety as the variety of disturbances it's trying to regulate: **"only variety can absorb variety."** Applied to governance, this becomes a question about whether a governance system's internal diversity of responses is sufficient to match the diversity of local conditions it's meant to serve — a single uniform national policy has low variety; a system that can differentiate its response by local circumstance has higher variety.

Your own site already uses this principle in the Systems Simulator: a centralized controller that observes only a national average and applies a uniform policy destroys spatial information, while a fractal/polycentric structure with nested local, regional and global controllers can cover more of the actual variety in the environment.

## Why this model matters for the Evidence programme

Requisite variety gives a formal grounding for a claim this project needs but can't establish empirically on its own: that there is, in principle, a *structural* reason to expect uniform central policy to underperform when local conditions vary a lot — not because central government is incompetent, but because a low-variety controller cannot fully represent a high-variety environment, regardless of how well-intentioned or well-resourced it is.

## What it establishes [R]

Within its formal model, requisite variety is a mathematically grounded control-theoretic result (Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, 1956), not something specific to governance — GAE applies it to governance as a domain, it doesn't derive it freshly from governance data.

## What it suggests [IP]

That governance architectures with more internal differentiation (more local decision points, more varied local responses) have more capacity, in principle, to match a diverse problem environment than architectures that force a single uniform response — relevant to why **[R1](/evidens/forskning/subsidiaritet-decentralisering/)** and **[R2](/evidens/forskning/skala-kapacitet-specialisering/)** treat local variation as potentially valuable rather than as noise to be minimized.

## What it does not establish

It does not establish that any *specific* Swedish governance arrangement has adequate or inadequate variety — that requires case-level empirical work, which is what the Cases section does. It also does not establish that more variety is always better: coordination costs (see [Coordination Tax, GAE V](/evidens/datamodeller/modeller/samordningsskatt/)) can offset variety gains, which is exactly the trade-off **[R7](/evidens/forskning/samordning-komplexitet-ansvar/)** investigates.

## Empirical relevance in this corpus

Used as an [IP] interpretive lens for **H1** (local information matching) and **H7** (functional scale variation) — not independently empirically tested by any case in the current corpus. **[Case S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/)**'s finding that the state's own 1961 reform documents recognized different functions needed different scales is a real-world pattern consistent with requisite variety reasoning, though the case itself doesn't test the formal model directly.
