# Health Rings

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Regular peer support groups for people living with a similar situation, for example long-term illness, loneliness in older age or becoming a parent for the first time. The groups are led by trained study circle leaders, often with their own experience, and are organised via study associations, patient associations and pensioners’ organisations. Health centres can refer patients to the groups.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Many people living with long-term illness or loneliness need more than healthcare can provide during a visit: someone to share experiences with, practical everyday advice and a sense of context. Such support already exists in patient associations, pensioners’ organisations and study associations, but the link to healthcare is often weak.

## How it would work

- **Regular meetings**, physically or digitally, for example every other week.
- **A trained study circle leader** leads the conversations and activities. The leader does not give medical advice.
- **The content** is based on the participants’ needs: exchange of experiences, walks, cooking, information from invited experts.
- **Link to healthcare:** the health centre can provide information about the groups and refer patients to them, and the groups know when and how participants should seek care.

**Examples of groups:**

| Type | Target group | Examples of content |
|-----|----------|-------------|
| Living with diabetes | People with type 2 diabetes | Exchange of experiences, walks, cooking, visits from a diabetes nurse |
| Older people and community | Older people who experience loneliness | Coffee, walks, reading aloud, help with digital technology |
| New parents | Parents with small children | Meetings, pram walks, conversations about everyday life |
| Long-term pain | People with long-term pain | Exchange of experiences and adapted movement, in cooperation with healthcare rehabilitation |

**An important boundary:** Changes in medication, diet for diabetes or treatment of pain must always be made in consultation with healthcare. The groups complement treatment but do not replace it.

**Most important assumption:** That peer support in groups improves well-being for people with long-term illness or loneliness, as a complement to healthcare.

- **Stop if:** participants change medication or treatment based on advice in the group without contact with healthcare, and the routines cannot prevent it.
- **Adjust if:** few of those referred by the health centre start participating.
- **Continue if:** participants report better well-being and more social support than the comparison group, without more unplanned healthcare contacts.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three municipalities of different types over three years, with groups in at least two areas per municipality, in cooperation with study associations, patient associations and health centres.

**Evaluation:** number of participants and how long they participate, change in self-rated well-being, participants’ experience of social support, and healthcare contacts compared with a comparison group.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Cost per municipality and year |
|------|---------------------------|
| Training and fee for study circle leaders | approx. SEK 100,000 |
| Premises and materials | approx. SEK 20,000 |
| Coordination and evaluation | approx. SEK 50,000 |

The study associations already have central government grants for study circles, which can cover part of the cost.

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Wellness allowance:** It should be investigated whether participation in such groups can count as wellness under the tax rules. This is not self-evident and may require amended rules.
2. **Personal data:** Information about participants’ health is sensitive. The groups should not keep records of participants’ health, and the evaluation should be based on voluntary, anonymous surveys.
3. **Responsibility:** The groups do not provide health and medical care. This needs to be clear to participants.

## Objections

**“The evidence is weak.”** Research on peer support shows positive effects for certain groups and conditions, but the results vary and the studies are often small. A pilot with evaluation is therefore proposed.

**“Laypeople may give incorrect advice.”** The study circle leaders should not give medical advice, and the groups should have a contact in healthcare.

**“It only reaches those who are already active.”** Referrals from the health centre and groups in areas with poorer health can broaden participation. The evaluation should monitor who participates.

## Swedish experience to build on

- **Patient associations**, such as diabetes, heart and lung associations, with local groups and peer support.
- **The Fontänhusen**, member-driven activities for people with mental ill-health.
- **Pensioners’ organisations’** walking groups and meetings.
- **Study associations**, with long experience of training study circle leaders.

## What you can do

**If you work at a health centre:** Make a list of patient associations and groups in the local area that staff can hand out.

**If you are active in a patient association or a study association:** Contact a health centre and tell them about your activities.

**If you live with a long-term illness:** Find out whether there is a patient association for your condition where you live.
