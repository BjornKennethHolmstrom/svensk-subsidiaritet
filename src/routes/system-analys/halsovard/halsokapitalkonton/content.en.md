# Health accounts

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A limited trial in which residents in one or a couple of areas receive a personal pot, for example SEK 2,000–3,000 per year, to use for preventive interventions such as smoking cessation, physical activity, dietary advice from a dietitian or participation in peer support groups. The trial should primarily investigate who uses the money and whether it affects health.

*This is a proposal to test in a limited trial with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Preventive interventions have difficulty finding space in healthcare. At the same time, many people pay themselves for things that can prevent ill-health. Employers can give employees a tax-free wellness allowance, but it does not reach those who lack work, for example the unemployed, pensioners and students.

## How it would work

- **An annual pot per resident** in the trial area, for example for people over 16.
- **A list of approved interventions**, decided on the basis of research on effects, for example smoking cessation, physical activity, fall prevention for older people and dietary advice from a registered dietitian.
- **Simple use**, via a digital service or a card, with the possibility of support for those who do not use digital services.
- **No savings function over several years in the trial**, to keep it simple and evaluable.

## An important problem to test

Experience from similar support suggests that those who already have good habits and good health often use such money most, while those with the greatest need use it least. A universal account can therefore increase health inequalities instead of reducing them. The trial should therefore compare two variants: one in which everyone receives the same amount, and one in which the amount is higher for groups with poorer health or lower income, or in which support is provided via health guides who help people use the money.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** One or two defined areas with different conditions, for example a smaller municipality and a city district, over three to four years, with a comparison group.

**Evaluation:** who uses the money and for what, change in lifestyle habits and self-rated health, healthcare contacts, and administrative costs.

## Legal issues

1. **Legal basis:** Such support likely requires a special decision on pilot activity, and it needs to be examined whether the region, municipality or state should be the responsible authority.
2. **Tax and benefits:** The support should be neither taxed nor affect other benefits, which may require legislative change.
3. **Personal data:** Information about what someone buys for their health is sensitive and must be handled with strong protection.
4. **Providers:** How providers are approved needs to be investigated, for example through a freedom of choice system.

## Objections

**“The money goes to those who are already healthy.”** That is the most important objection, and the reason why the trial should compare different variants.

**“It is expensive.”** A universal account for all residents would cost a great deal. The trial should show whether the cost is proportionate to the benefit before anything larger is considered.

**“Singapore and the USA have health accounts.”** These systems, such as Medisave in Singapore and Health Savings Accounts in the USA, are primarily savings accounts for paying for healthcare, not support for preventive interventions, and are therefore not directly comparable.

## What you can do

**If you are a regional politician:** Investigate which preventive interventions the region already offers, and which groups use them.

**If you are a researcher:** Investigate whether there is an opportunity to evaluate such a trial with a comparison group.
