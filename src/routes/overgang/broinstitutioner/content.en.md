# Bridge Institutions

## The infrastructure that makes the valley shallow

**[The Transition Graph](/overgang/overgangsgraf/)** shows which capabilities need to be built. **[The Transition Valley](/overgang/overgangsdalen/)** shows what happens if they aren't built in time. This page describes the six concrete bridge institutions themselves — the layer that sits *underneath* the whole graph.

---

## B1 — Evidence infrastructure

Independent capacity for scientific evidence, forecasting, impact assessment, systems modelling and uncertainty analysis.

This allows more actors to participate in decision-making **without pluralism turning into epistemic chaos**. A national systems-evidence body could provide evidence synthesis, scenario modelling and policy evaluation — advising rather than governing.

---

## B2 — Deliberative infrastructure

Citizens' assemblies, deliberative mini-publics, structured stakeholder processes.

This means increased pluralism doesn't just become "everyone shouting louder." It becomes **structured information aggregation** — a permanent capacity rather than ad hoc consultations, with clear channels for Parliament to refer complex or long-term questions to deliberation.

---

## B3 — Coordination infrastructure (cross-scale)

Mechanisms connecting municipality ↔ region ↔ nation ↔ international **before** major powers are shifted downward.

This is perhaps the single most important transition technology. Cross-sector coordination councils, shared strategic models and regular cross-sector crisis exercises help the state start learning to coordinate **networks** of institutions, not just ministries.

---

## B4 — Accountability infrastructure

Every delegated authority should have: **mandate → measurable responsibility → transparency → review → appeal → sanction/removal.**

Without this, subsidiarity becomes **responsibility diffusion**. With it, subsidiarity can become **responsibility localization**. That distinction is enormous — it's the difference between distributing power and distributing unaccountability.

---

## B5 — Interoperability infrastructure

Different institutions must be able to share data, share standards, resolve conflicts, recognize each other's decisions and coordinate emergencies.

This is effectively the **network protocol layer** of governance. Polycentricity only works well when the nodes have protocols — otherwise "autonomous institutions" just become **isolated** institutions.

---

## B6 — Meta-governance

Finally: who decides whether the relationships between all these institutions are actually working?

This is the **control layer of the control system**. Not a super-government, but the layer that monitors the architecture itself and facilitates adaptation. See **[Meta-Governance →](/arkitektur/meta-styrning/)** for a full treatment.

The transition, then, is not:

> centralized → decentralized → polycentric

It is:

> **centralized coordination → distributed capability → distributed authority → integrated polycentricity.**

## Why the "boring" reforms may be the most important

A reform called "create an independent national systems-evidence capacity" sounds bureaucratic. But it may be decisive, because it enables **better deliberation → better experiments → better subsidiarity decisions → better polycentric governance.**

Likewise, "create common cross-institutional accountability protocols" sounds bureaucratic. But it may be a prerequisite for safely transferring substantial authority.

That means **the most strategic reforms are often not the most visibly transformative ones.** That's a very "Governance as Engineering" result.
