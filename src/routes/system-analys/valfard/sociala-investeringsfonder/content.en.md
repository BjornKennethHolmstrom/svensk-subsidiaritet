# Social investment funds

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Funds or special resources that finance preventive interventions, where the results are followed up closely and where the activities that achieve lower costs thanks to the intervention can contribute to refilling the fund. Several Swedish municipalities and regions have worked in this way since the early 2010s. The proposal is to build on their experiences and to test cooperation between municipality, region and state.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Public budgets are annual and divided between activities. A preventive intervention costs money today, while the benefit comes later and often for other actors. An intervention for young people at risk can reduce costs for social services, healthcare, the police and the prison and probation service, but none of them alone has reason to pay for it.

## What already exists

Social investment funds or special resources for social investments have been established in a large number of Swedish municipalities and regions. Some early examples are the Municipality of Umeå, which allocated SEK 120 million for the period 2011–2014, and the Municipality of Norrköping, where activities that achieved lower costs thanks to an intervention returned funds to the fund. The economist Lars Hultkrantz has reviewed the experiences in a report for SNS (2015), and the Expert Group on Public Economics has in a report (ESO 2020:1) proposed, among other things, a state outcome fund.

## How it would work

1. **Joint fund:** Municipalities, the region and possibly the state allocate resources to a joint fund for a county or a region.
2. **Interventions with clear follow-up:** The fund finances interventions supported by research or proven experience, and with a plan for how the effects are to be measured.
3. **Recycling:** If an intervention leads to lower costs for a participating actor, part of the saving is returned to the fund.
4. **Learning:** The results are reported openly, even when interventions do not work.

**Examples of interventions:** parenting support and early interventions for families, individual support to work for people with mental ill-health (so-called supported employment), debt counselling and coordinated interventions for young people who are neither working nor studying.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Two or three regional funds where the region and municipalities join together, over five years.

**Evaluation:** effects for the target groups, how much of the estimated savings actually materialised, and whether the working method has changed cooperation between the actors.

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Budgeting and accounting:** Municipal accounting makes it difficult to handle investments in social interventions in the same way as investments in buildings. The issue has been investigated, among other places in the ESO report.
2. **Recycling between responsible authorities:** Recycling within a municipality is simpler than between municipality, region and state. The forms for this need to be investigated.
3. **Private capital:** So-called social outcome contracts, where private investors get their money back if the intervention succeeds, have been tested in other countries and on a small scale in Sweden. They are contested, and the proposal here is primarily based on public funds.

## Objections

**“The savings are difficult to measure.”** That is true. The experiences from Swedish funds show that it is often difficult to show that a saving actually arose and where. The evaluation needs to be realistic about what can be measured.

**“It becomes a way of shifting costs.”** Clear agreements on who contributes what, and on recycling, are needed.

**“Private investors should not profit from social problems.”** Outcome contracts with private capital are contested. The internationally best-known example, a bond to reduce recidivism among released prisoners in Peterborough in the United Kingdom, was reported to give a certain reduction in recidivism, but the experiences from other projects are mixed.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal commissioner or regional commissioner:** Find out whether the municipality or region has social investment resources, how they have been used and what the evaluations have shown.

**If you are a civil servant:** Identify a preventive intervention that would reduce costs for several activities, and calculate who would gain from it.

**If you are a researcher:** Offer support in evaluating existing funds, so that the experiences can be used in new ones.
