# Meta-Governance

## The governance of governance

When a society consists of many governance nodes — local, regional, national, sectoral, deliberative, expert-based — a new question arises:

> **Who ensures that the governance systems work together?**

This is not the same as a "super-government." Meta-governance is the capacity to:

- detect gaps and overlaps between institutions
- handle conflicts between levels and domains
- improve coordination
- follow up on how the allocation of responsibility actually works
- pilot new institutional solutions
- change the governance architecture when it no longer fits reality

---

## A constitutional first principle

> **No allocation of governance authority is presumed permanently optimal.**

Instead, a continuous loop applies:

**monitor → evaluate → detect mismatch → redesign → test → adapt.**

That turns institutional reform into a **normal feedback function of the system**, rather than a constitutional crisis that happens once a generation.

---

## Corrigible governance

An institution shouldn't merely be capable of making decisions. It should be capable of **recognizing when its own decision architecture is starting to fail.** This may be the strongest bridge between Swedish reform work and a deeper, GGF-like architecture — and it holds regardless of how far one otherwise wants to move along the spectrum.

---

## Five functions

**Governance performance monitoring** — detect coordination failures, jurisdictional gaps, duplicated authority, accountability gaps, systematic externalities and under-resourced institutions.

**Periodic architecture review** — regularly ask: is this allocation of authority still optimal?

**Conflict-resolution protocols** — clear escalation paths between institutions when they come into conflict.

**Governance experimentation** — allow institutional arrangements to be piloted and evaluated before they're scaled up.

**Constitutional learning** — enable controlled institutional adaptation without every change requiring a systemic crisis.

---

## A fifth decentralization

Beyond decentering the political, the epistemic and the territorial, there is a fifth move: decentering **agency over adaptation** itself — letting society participate in deciding **how governance should evolve**, not just who should govern within a given architecture.

That is the difference between choosing a government and having **constitutional agency**.

---

## Why this isn't a utopian leap

Meta-governance doesn't need to be introduced as an abstract endpoint. It can be introduced step by step, in entirely everyday terms:

> "We currently lack a mechanism for regularly testing whether our own governance arrangements still work."

That is a fully comprehensible, defensible reform in its own right — whether or not one shares the broader vision of polycentric, adaptive governance. The destination doesn't have to be hidden. The capability dependencies simply need to determine the order in which reforms happen.
