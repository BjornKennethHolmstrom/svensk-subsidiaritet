# Safety centres

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Local, open meeting places where the police, social services, field assistants, mediators, youth activities and advice services are under the same roof. The idea is to lower the threshold for seeking help or providing information, and to give those who work with safety in an area a shared base.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

The police reform’s goal of a police force close to citizens has not been achieved. At the same time, social services, schools, leisure activities, housing companies and the police often work separately in the same area, with their own premises, opening hours and contact routes. For those who live there, it can be difficult to know where to turn, and for those who work there, it can be difficult to coordinate.

A safety centre is a way to bring those activities together physically, so that it becomes easier to meet and cooperate, and so that the police become part of an everyday place rather than a separate authority.

## How it would work

**Principles:**
- **Open and accessible:** an entrance without a security check for the public areas, and opening hours in the evenings as well.
- **Mixed staffing:** the police are present, but the majority of staff work with social and preventive work. The proposal is that police officers make up about one third.
- **Separate functions where needed:** the police’s work areas and social services’ conversation rooms are separate, for reasons of safety and confidentiality.

**Possible content:**
- a police reception for reports and contact with community police officers
- rooms for mediation and conversations
- youth activities after school
- advice on, for example, rent, debts and contacts with authorities
- social services on site on certain days
- a coordinator who knows the area’s resources and can guide people onward
- a room that associations, neighbourhood watch groups and Safety Rings can book

### What we know

- The goal of the 2015 police reform of a police force closer to citizens has not been achieved, according to Statskontoret’s evaluations.
- The proportion with high confidence in the police increased from 50 percent in 2015 to 62 percent in 2025, according to Brå’s NTU.
- The police’s situational picture for 2025 covers 65 vulnerable areas, 19 of which are particularly vulnerable.

### What we assume

- That co-location lowers the threshold for residents to seek help and provide information.
- That it becomes easier for the police, social services and leisure activities to cooperate if they are under the same roof.
- That residents will use the centre even though the police are there.

### What we do not know

- **How the whole behaves.** Police, social services and youth activities in the same place can create a confidence that none of them gains on its own. But the combination can also harm social services: if residents perceive social services and the police as the same thing, they may avoid seeking support. A visible centre can also become a target for threats or vandalism.
- Whether the confidentiality rules in practice make cooperation on site harder than expected.

### How we would test it

- **Test:** three to five centres over three years, with comparison areas.
- **Stop if:** voluntary contacts with social services decrease in the pilot areas compared with the comparison areas, which may indicate that co-location harms confidence in social services; or if serious security incidents recur.
- **Adjust if:** the centre is used almost only for police cases; or if staff in the follow-up describe cooperation as unchanged.
- **Continue if:** the centre is used broadly by residents of different ages, confidence in the police and municipality develops better than in the comparison areas, and staff assess that cooperation has improved.

The threshold values are to be determined before the pilot starts and not changed afterwards.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Around ten centres over three years, in areas where the municipality’s situational picture and the police’s situational picture of vulnerable areas show a need.

**Selection:** The locations are chosen jointly by the Swedish Police Authority, the municipality and local actors. Criteria may be that there are suitable premises, that the police and municipality already cooperate, for example through citizen pledges, and that there are local associations that want to participate.

**Organisation:**
- premises from the municipality, a housing company or the Swedish Police Authority
- a joint steering group with the police, the municipality and civil society
- each activity is financed by its responsible authority: the police by the Swedish Police Authority, social services and leisure by the municipality, advice and mediation by the municipality or non-profit organisations

**Staffing (proposal for an area with around 20,000 residents):**

| Function | Staff (FTE) |
|----------|----------------------------|
| Community police officers | 4 |
| Mediators | 1 |
| Youth activities | 2 |
| Social services | 1 |
| Advice | 0.5 |
| Coordinator | 1 |
| Host and administration | 1.5 |
| **Total** | **approx. 11** |

**Evaluation:** number of visits, who visits the centre, change in perceived safety and confidence in the area compared with similar areas, how cooperation between the activities works, and how staff experience the work.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | One-time cost per centre | Annual cost per centre |
|------|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| Adaptation of premises | SEK 5–10 million | – |
| Furnishings and equipment | approx. SEK 1 million | – |
| Staff | – | approx. SEK 9 million |
| Rent and operations | – | approx. SEK 1.5 million |
| Coordination | – | approx. SEK 1 million |
| **Total per centre** | **SEK 6–11 million** | **approx. SEK 11.5 million** |

A large part of the staff cost relates to staff who are already in the area. The actual additional cost depends on how much is new.

## Legal issues to investigate

1. **Exchange of information:** The police and social services are covered by different confidentiality rules. There are already provisions that permit certain exchanges of information to prevent crime, especially concerning young people, but co-location does not change confidentiality. It should be investigated whether the current rules are sufficient.
2. **Responsible authority:** The centre is a co-location, not a new authority. Responsibility for each activity remains with its responsible authority.
3. **Premises and procurement:** How premises are rented out and how non-profit organisations are engaged must follow the rules on procurement and state aid.

## Objections

**“The police should not run youth centres.”** They should not, either. The police are one of several activities in the centre, and youth activities are run by the municipality or associations.

**“Residents will not go in if the police are there.”** That is possible, and therefore the public areas should be usable without contact with the police. Confidence in the police has increased in recent years, but is lower in certain groups and areas.

**“The mixing of roles may harm confidence in social services.”** If residents perceive social services and the police as the same thing, it may become harder for social services to reach those who need support. Clear boundaries between the activities are needed.

**“It is expensive.”** The cost depends on how much is new staff and how much is existing activities moving together.

## Swedish experience to build on

- **Citizen pledges:** local agreements between the police and the municipality on joint initiatives.
- **Citizen offices and service offices**, where several authorities and municipal activities are in the same place.
- **Field assistants and youth centres**, which already work in the areas but often separately from the police.
- **Community police officers**, introduced with the 2015 reform to strengthen local presence.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician or police chief:** Map which activities already exist in an area and investigate whether any of them can be co-located as a first step.

**If you are a property owner or housing company:** Investigate whether there are premises that can be used for a smaller pilot.

**If you are an association leader:** Contact the municipality and the local police and describe what need exists and what your association can contribute.
