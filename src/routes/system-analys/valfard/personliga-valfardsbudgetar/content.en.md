# Personal welfare budgets

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** For people who have received social assistance for a long time: longer decision periods, coordinated payments and better opportunities to work without losing all support. The goal is less administration for both the individual and social services, and stronger incentives to take work.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Social assistance is normally assessed month by month. The applicant submits documentation on income and expenses each time, and social services make a new decision. For someone with a long-term and stable need, this means a great deal of administration on both sides. The support is paid out in money, and the individual already decides today how it is used within the budget it covers.

The biggest problem for many is the marginal effects. Since social assistance in principle decreases with every krona earned, it pays poorly to take a part-time job. For those who have received social assistance for a longer time, there has been an employment incentive since 2014, where part of the earned income is not counted for a period. It helps, but does not solve the whole problem, especially when housing allowance and other benefits are also affected.

## How it would work

For participants who have received social assistance for a longer time, for example at least one year:

- **Longer decision periods:** decisions for, for example, six months at a time, with an obligation to report changed circumstances.
- **A single amount:** the support is calculated as a fixed monthly amount based on household size and housing cost, without monthly reporting of expenses.
- **Better incentives to work:** a larger share of earned income is disregarded, or for a longer period, than with today’s employment incentive.
- **Support with finances for those who want it:** access to budget and debt counselling.

Participation is voluntary, and those who do not want to participate receive ordinary processing.

**An option that should be tested cautiously:** paying the support onto a card where certain purchases are blocked, for example gambling. That would mean less freedom than today, since the support is currently paid out in money. It should therefore, in that case, only be used if the individual themselves wants it.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** A few thousand participants in ten municipalities over three years, with random selection among those who want to participate and a comparison group that receives ordinary processing.

**Evaluation:** proportion who enter work, incomes, health and perceived quality of life, administrative costs, and the cost of social assistance.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Assumption | Cost per year |
|------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Increased employment incentive | depends on how many start working | uncertain, likely tens of millions SEK |
| Budget and debt counselling | 10 municipalities | approx. SEK 5 million |
| Evaluation | | approx. SEK 3 million |

Whether the pilot becomes cheaper or more expensive than the current system depends on how many enter work and how much administration is saved. That is precisely what is to be evaluated.

## Legal issues

1. **Decision periods and calculation:** It should be investigated what the new Social Services Act already permits, and what requires legislative change.
2. **Employment incentive:** A strengthened employment incentive in a pilot activity likely requires legal support.
3. **Coordination with other benefits:** Housing allowance and other benefits are administered by the Swedish Social Insurance Agency and affect the marginal effects. Coordination between municipality and state is needed.

## Objections

**“It will become more expensive.”** Possibly, at least in the short term. But if more people start working, it can become cheaper over time. That is an empirical question.

**“Municipalities lose control.”** The obligation to report changed circumstances remains, as does the possibility of checks and recovery.

**“It is unfair to those who do not get to participate.”** A randomised pilot is the fairest way to find out whether a change works before it is introduced for everyone.

## Experience to build on

- **Finland’s basic income experiment 2017–2018**, where unemployed people received an unconditional payment, had small effects on employment but improved well-being.
- **Today’s employment incentive in social assistance**, which can be evaluated and developed.
- **Universal Credit in the United Kingdom**, which coordinated several benefits but was criticised for, among other things, long waiting times and administrative problems, showing the risks of large system reforms.

## What you can do

**If you are a unit manager in social services:** Investigate how often long-term recipients have to submit the same documentation, and whether longer decision periods are possible within current rules.

**If you are a municipal politician:** Propose an evaluation of how the employment incentive is used in the municipality, and a pilot with longer decision periods.
