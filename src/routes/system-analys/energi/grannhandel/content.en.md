# Energy sharing between neighbours

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Pilot projects in which households, tenant-owner associations and small companies share electricity from solar cells or other local production with one another, based on the new rules on energy sharing. The aim is to test how sharing works in practice, who benefits from it and how it affects the electricity grid.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## What applies today

- **Sale of surplus electricity:** Those who have solar cells sell their surplus to an electricity trading company, not to the grid company. The grid company pays a smaller compensation for the benefit the feed-in provides to the grid. Earlier versions of this page claimed that the surplus is sold to the grid company. That is not correct.
- **The tax reduction** for micro-production, 60 öre per kilowatt-hour, was abolished from 1 January 2026. This makes it more profitable to use one’s own electricity directly or to share it.
- **Sharing within and between nearby buildings** has been possible since 2022 in certain types of local grids that do not require a grid concession.
- **Energy sharing over the electricity grid:** The government has presented proposals for rules on energy sharing, in which households, small and medium-sized companies and public bodies are to be able to use electricity from an installation they own or rent at another location, or to which someone else has transferred the right (prop. 2025/26:240).

Earlier versions of this page proposed a separate exemption in the Electricity Act for neighbour trade. With the new rules on energy sharing, it is instead these that should be tested.

## How the pilots would work

- **A defined area**, for example a villa area, a village or a few tenant-owner associations.
- **Sharing agreements** between those who produce and those who use the electricity, according to the new rules.
- **Metering and billing** via existing electricity meters and the grid company’s systems.
- **Follow-up** of how much electricity is shared, what it means economically for the participants and how it affects the local grid.

**What we know:** The tax reduction for micro-production was abolished from January 2026, and the government has presented proposals for rules on energy sharing. How the rules are designed in detail determines how profitable sharing becomes.

**Most important assumption:** That energy sharing makes local production available to more people, for example tenants and others without their own roof, and not only to those who already have solar cells.

- **Stop if:** sharing in practice takes place almost only between households that already have good finances, while grid costs are shifted to other customers.
- **Adjust if:** few participate because of the administration around metering and agreements.
- **Continue if:** participation is broad, including among those without their own production, and the effects on the grid are manageable.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three to five areas of different types over two to three years, in cooperation with grid companies, electricity trading companies and the Swedish Energy Agency.

**Evaluation:** participants’ costs and revenues, who participates, administrative costs and effects on the grid.

## Legal and practical issues

1. **The new rules:** How the rules on energy sharing are designed in detail, for example regarding taxes and grid fees on shared electricity, determines how profitable sharing becomes. The question of energy tax and grid fee on shared electricity has been debated among consultation bodies.
2. **Balance responsibility:** Someone must be responsible for ensuring that planned and actual production and consumption match. This also applies to sharing.
3. **Personal data:** Data about households’ electricity use is personal data.

## Objections

**“It benefits those who have their own solar cells.”** Sharing can also let tenants and others without their own roof share in local production. The evaluation should monitor the distribution.

**“Grid costs are shifted to other customers.”** If shared electricity avoids certain fees, the costs of the grid can be distributed among fewer. That is a real trade-off.

## What you can do

**If you live in a tenant-owner association:** Investigate the possibility of sharing solar electricity within the property or with nearby buildings.

**If you work at a grid company or electricity trading company:** Prepare pilot projects that can start once the rules on energy sharing are in place.
