# Evidence

## We don't assume decentralization is always better

Svensk Subsidiaritet builds a **[governance architecture](/arkitektur/)** and a **[transition strategy](/overgang/)**. But an architecture is only a hypothesis until it's tested against reality.

Evidence is the empirical and analytical layer of the project. Its purpose isn't to argue that decentralization is good. Its purpose is to investigate:

> **Under what conditions does a particular distribution of decision-making power, information, resources and responsibility produce better system outcomes?**

It should be possible to move from **question → research → case → mechanism → evidence → synthesis → uncertainty → next question**. Evidence should be able to change the project's hypotheses and architecture — not just illustrate them. That's why this section presents both supporting and contradictory evidence.

---

## Why we investigate this

It's easy to pick a governance level on intuition or ideology: "decisions should be made close to people" or "some things need national coordination." Both can be true — depending on which mechanism is actually at work.

Until we test the relevant mechanism, we don't know which one applies to a given question. The Evidence section exists to make that test systematic, rather than assuming the answer in advance.

---

## Our approach

```text
Research → Cases → Data & Models → Synthesis
```

**[Research](/evidens/research/)** synthesizes what the literature currently says about nine broad empirical questions — one page per question, not one page per theoretical paper.

**[Cases](/evidens/fall/)** reconstruct what actually happened in real Swedish (and eventually Nordic and international) governance reforms — beyond labels like "decentralization."

**[Data & Models](/evidens/datamodeller/)** documents how we operationalize otherwise abstract concepts, and how the Governance as Engineering (GAE) models connect to our empirical questions.

**[Synthesis](/evidens/syntes/)** brings it all together in an evidence matrix, with explicit counterevidence and explicit unknowns.

---

## What we're asking right now

Some of the questions driving the work:

> Under what conditions does local decision-making improve information use?

> When do economies of scale outweigh the benefits of local adaptation?

> When does decentralization create externalities or coordination failure instead of solving them?

> When is polycentric governance more resilient than centralized governance?

> Which institutional architectures preserve the capacity to learn and adapt?

See the full, living list under **[Our Questions →](/evidens/sa-vet-vi/fragor/)**.

## Current state of evidence — briefly

The full picture lives in the **[Evidence Matrix](/evidens/syntes/matris/)**, but a few carefully qualified observations already:

- The evidence is more consistent with a **conditional "governance fit" proposition** than a universal decentralization principle.
- **Formal decentralization is not a reliable proxy** for decisions actually moving closer to those affected.
- The appropriate governance scale **varies substantially by function** rather than following one universal administrative level.
- Some benefits associated with larger scale may **potentially be achieved through shared financing or shared functions**, without merging the underlying political jurisdictions.

These are preliminary, revisable assessments — not conclusions.
