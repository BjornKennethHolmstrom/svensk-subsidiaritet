# Education: who decides over the school?

## A school system that is already unusually decentralised

The Swedish school system underwent major changes in the early 1990s. In 1991, the municipalities took over responsibility for schools from the state, and in 1992 the independent school reform was introduced, in which independent schools are funded with a school voucher on the same terms as municipal schools. Since then, Sweden has had one of the most decentralised school systems among OECD countries, combined with national curricula, national tests and state supervision through the Swedish Schools Inspectorate.

The question of whether the state should take back more responsibility has come up again and again. A state inquiry, *The State’s Responsibility for Schools* (SOU 2022:53), presented two alternatives: a complete nationalisation, in which the state becomes the responsible authority, and a system in which the state strengthens its responsibility without taking over the role of responsible authority.

## What the results show

In the PISA surveys, Sweden had its best result in the first measurement in 2000. The results fell to a low point in 2012 and then rose again. In PISA 2022, results in mathematics and reading declined, as in most other countries, but Swedish pupils still performed above the OECD average in reading, mathematics and science. Earlier versions of this page claimed that Sweden is below the OECD average. That is not correct.

The clearest weakness concerns equivalence. According to the Swedish National Agency for Education, equivalence in Sweden has deteriorated and is worse than in the other Nordic countries. This means that a pupil’s background and which school the pupil attends play a greater role than before.

## The cybernetic reading

Pupils, schools and municipalities differ greatly. A school needs to be able to adapt teaching to its pupils. At the same time, all pupils need to receive the same basic knowledge, and grades must mean the same thing across the whole country for selection to further studies to be fair.

- **Strengthen capacity locally:** teachers’ professional discretion, the school’s opportunities to adapt teaching and cooperation with the local community.
- **Dampen variation where equivalence requires it:** common syllabuses, national tests as support for grading and a common teacher certification.

Swedish schools can be said to have tried both at the same time: great freedom for the responsible authorities but detailed steering of what is to be assessed. The discussion about nationalisation shows that many believe the variation between responsible authorities has become too great, rather than too small. This is an important objection to a simple subsidiarity reading.

## Remaining problems

- **Equivalence.** The differences between schools and between pupils with different backgrounds have increased.
- **Grading.** Several studies have shown signs of grade inflation and differences in how schools set grades, which affects fairness in selection to upper secondary school and higher education.
- **Teacher supply.** There is a shortage of qualified teachers in many subjects and parts of the country.
- **Cooperation with the surrounding society.** Schools often have weak contacts with working life, associational life and cultural life, especially outside vocational programmes.

## Subsidiarity-based proposals

The proposals are ideas to test in limited pilots with evaluation. Each proposal has its own page.

| Proposal | Brief description |
|--------|------------------|
| [Learning Rings](./laranderingar) | Recurring projects in which pupils work together with professionals and other knowledgeable people from the local community, under the guidance of teachers. |
| [Local profiles in teaching](./kommunal-laroplansautonomi) | A trial in which schools and municipalities are given greater scope to link parts of teaching to local conditions, within the framework of national knowledge requirements. |
| [Portfolios as assessment material](./portfoljbaserad-bedomning) | Pupil portfolios as a complement to tests, as a basis for the teacher’s grading and in the alternative selection to higher education. |
| [Teacher cooperatives](./lararkooperativ) | Teachers who together run a school as an economic association, within the framework of the Education Act’s rules for independent schools. |
| [Competence banks](./kompetensbanker) | A local brokerage of guest contributions from professionals and other knowledgeable people, as a complement to teachers’ teaching. |

## Objections

| Objection | How the proposals need to address it |
|---------|------|
| More local freedom can increase inequality | The proposals must not weaken pupils’ right to the same basic knowledge. The evaluation should particularly monitor pupils with weaker circumstances. |
| Teachers are trained for a reason | The proposals complement teachers’ teaching. The teacher always has responsibility for teaching and grading. |
| More responsible authorities create more fragmentation | Teacher cooperatives are covered by the same supervision and requirements as other independent schools. If the evaluation shows problems, they should not be spread. |

## Conclusion

Swedish schools are not a centralised system that needs to be decentralised, but one of the most decentralised among OECD countries, where the debate is rather about whether the state needs to take greater responsibility for equivalence. Subsidiarity here does not mean moving more power downwards in general, but distinguishing between what needs to be common, such as knowledge requirements and grades, and what can be adapted locally, such as working methods, cooperation with the local community and teachers’ professional space. The proposals here concern the latter.

### Three things you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Find out how the municipality’s schools cooperate with local working life and associational life, and what prevents more cooperation.

**If you are a teacher or head teacher:** Invite a professional from the local area to a recurring project, and link it to the syllabus’s goals.

**If you are a parent:** Ask the school how it works with equivalence, and how it uses its local scope, for example the school’s choice.
