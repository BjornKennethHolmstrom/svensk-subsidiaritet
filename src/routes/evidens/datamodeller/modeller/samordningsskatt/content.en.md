# Coordination Tax (GAE V)

**Full report:** [Paper V: The Coordination Failure Tax](https://www.bjornkennethholmstrom.org/working-papers/coordination-failure-tax)
**Simulation / code:** [gae-governance-simulator](https://github.com/BjornKennethHolmstrom/gae-governance-simulator)

---

## What it's about

The coordination tax is the cost a governance system pays — in delay, duplicated effort, inconsistent decisions, or outright conflict — when authority is distributed across multiple actors who must nonetheless act coherently. It's the formal counterweight to [Requisite Variety (GAE IV)](/evidens/datamodeller/modeller/nodvandig-variation/): variety without coordination doesn't automatically produce a better-adapted system, it can also just produce fragmentation.

## Why this model matters for the Evidence programme

Every case in this corpus that distributes a function across more than one actor pays some version of this tax. It gives the project a name and a formal frame for something the cases show repeatedly but can't yet quantify: distributing authority is not free, and the size of the cost depends on the coordination architecture around the distributed actors, not just on how many actors there are.

## What it establishes [R]

Within its formal model, that coordination costs are a real, structural feature of distributed decision-making, not merely a symptom of bad management — a model-level result about the relationship between the number/independence of decision nodes and the cost of keeping their actions consistent.

## What it suggests [IP]

That the size of a governance system's coordination tax can be reduced by deliberate bridging infrastructure (shared information, joint decision bodies, protocols) rather than being fixed once the number of actors is fixed — directly relevant to why **[Ädelreformen (S3)](/evidens/fall/sverige/adelreformen/)** and **[intermunicipal cooperation (S5)](/evidens/fall/sverige/mellankommunal-samverkan/)** both show coordination problems easing once bridging mechanisms (joint committees, information-transfer regulations, trust-building) were built.

## What it does not establish

It does not give a validated way to *measure* the coordination tax in a real Swedish case — the corpus's cases document coordination problems qualitatively (persistent boundary issues in S3, accountability-tracking difficulty in S5) rather than quantifying a "tax" in the model's terms. It also does not establish the exchange rate between variety gained and coordination cost paid — that trade-off is observed directionally in the cases, not measured.

## Empirical relevance in this corpus

Used as an [IP] interpretive lens for **H6** (spillovers increase the value of coordination), **H8** (cooperation without merger), and **H14** (boundary fit) — see **[R7 — Coordination, Complexity & Accountability](/evidens/forskning/samordning-komplexitet-ansvar/)** for the fullest treatment of how this plays out across the case corpus.
