# Restorative justice

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** For less serious crimes, where the offender has admitted the offence and the victim wants to participate, a mediation meeting can lead to an agreement on an apology and compensation. If the agreement is fulfilled, the prosecutor can take it into account in the decision on prosecution. The proposal builds on the mediation in connection with crime that already exists in Swedish law and extends it from mainly young people to more offenders.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

In an ordinary legal process, the victim often has a limited role, and the offender rarely faces the consequences of their action directly. Restorative justice starts from the premise that a crime harms people and relationships, and that the harm should as far as possible be repaired. Research reviews, for example Sherman and Strang (2007), have found that mediation meetings between victim and offender often produce high satisfaction among victims and in several studies a certain reduction in recidivism. However, the effects vary between types of crime and programmes.

## What already exists

- **The Act on Mediation in Connection with Crime** (2002:445) regulates how mediation is carried out. Mediation is voluntary for both parties.
- **Municipalities must offer mediation** to young offenders.
- **Waiver of prosecution:** the prosecutor may in certain cases refrain from prosecuting, for example for minor offences (Code of Judicial Procedure, Chapter 20, Section 7). Special rules exist for young people.
- **Sentencing:** the fact that the offender has tried to compensate for or limit the harm can be taken into account when the penalty is determined.

Mediation is nevertheless used to a limited extent, and is today mainly aimed at young people.

## The proposal

**Conditions:**
- the offender has admitted the offence, or the evidence is clear
- the victim wants to participate, entirely voluntarily
- the crime is of a less serious kind, where the penalty would likely be a fine or a conditional sentence
- a trained, impartial mediator leads the process

**The process:**
1. The prosecutor assesses that the case is suitable and refers it to a mediation service.
2. The mediator meets the parties separately and explains what mediation involves. Both must say yes.
3. The parties meet under the mediator’s guidance, in a neutral venue.
4. If they reach an agreement, for example on an apology, compensation or work, it is written down.
5. The mediator follows up that the agreement is fulfilled.
6. The prosecutor takes the result into account in the decision on prosecution. If the agreement is not fulfilled, or if either party withdraws, the case is handled as usual.

**Crimes that may be included (proposal):** petty theft, minor damage, minor assault without serious injury, less serious cases of unlawful threat without weapons, arbitrary conduct.

**Crimes that always take the usual route:** violence with weapons, aggravated assault, sexual offences, crimes in close relationships, crimes against children and repeated serious crime. Crimes in close relationships are excluded because the power imbalance between the parties makes mediation unsuitable.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three regions over three years, with a few municipalities in each.

**Organisation:**
- The Swedish Prosecution Authority trains prosecutors in assessing which cases are suitable
- mediation services are expanded from those the municipalities already have for young people, in cooperation with, for example, Victim Support Sweden
- the mediators undergo special training (proposal: 80 hours)
- an independent group follows up quality and handles complaints

**Evaluation:** compare with similar cases handled in the usual way, with regard to victims’ experience of redress, the proportion of agreements fulfilled, recidivism after one and two years, processing time and cost.

## Legal issues to investigate

1. **Link to prosecution:** It should be investigated whether the current rules on waiver of prosecution are sufficient, or whether an explicit provision is needed that a fulfilled mediation agreement should be taken into account.
2. **Mediation for adults:** The Mediation Act already applies regardless of age, but resources and routines exist mainly for young people. A pilot primarily requires funding and organisation, not necessarily new law.
3. **Confidentiality:** What is said during mediation needs to be protected, while the duty to report serious crimes against children remains.

## Objections

**“That is letting criminals go free.”** Mediation requires the offender to take responsibility and compensate for the harm. It does not replace punishment for serious crimes. But it goes against the recent direction in Swedish criminal policy, with stricter penalties and reduced sentence discounts for young people, and that tension should be reported openly.

**“The victim may feel pressured.”** Participation must be entirely voluntary, and the victim must be able to withdraw at any time without consequences for the case.

**“The offender pretends to regret it.”** An agreement requires concrete actions that are followed up. If it is not fulfilled, the case is handled as usual.

**“Prosecutors will not use the possibility.”** Without training and clear routines, that is a real risk.

## Experience to build on

- **Mediation in connection with crime in Sweden**, which municipalities offer to young offenders.
- **New Zealand** has since 1989 used so-called family group conferences for young offenders.
- **The Norwegian Mediation Service (Konfliktrådet)** is a state mediation service that, among other things, handles mediation in connection with crime and enforces penalties for young people.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician or civil servant:** Examine how often the municipality’s mediation service is used today, and what would be required for it to receive more cases.

**If you work in the justice system:** Find out how mediation is used in your area and what obstacles exist.

**If you are a lawyer, social worker or similar:** Investigate the possibility of training as a mediator.
