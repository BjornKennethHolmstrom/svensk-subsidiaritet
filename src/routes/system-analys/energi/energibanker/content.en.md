# Local flexibility

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** A local service, run by a municipality, a cooperative or a company, that gathers the flexibility of many households and companies, such as charging of electric cars, heat pumps, water heaters and batteries, and offers it to the grid company when the grid is heavily loaded. Participants receive compensation, and the grid company can in some cases postpone expensive grid reinforcements.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Electricity grids are dimensioned for the hours when the load is highest, often cold winter evenings. If some of the consumption can be moved a few hours, the grid can handle more without being expanded as quickly. Many households have equipment that can be controlled without comfort being noticeably affected.

## What already exists

- **Svenska kraftnät** buys ancillary services and flexibility to maintain balance in the entire electricity system.
- **Local flexibility markets** have been tested, among other places in the Stockholm region, where grid companies have bought flexibility to handle capacity shortages.
- **Electricity trading companies and other actors** already offer controlled charging and control of heat pumps based on hourly prices.

## How it would work

- **The participants** register their equipment and set limits, for example that the car must be charged to a certain level by a certain time, or that the temperature must not fall below a certain level.
- **The service** controls the equipment when the grid company or the market signals high load.
- **The compensation** is distributed to the participants.
- **Equipment that affects safety or health**, such as freezers or medical equipment, is never controlled.

**Most important assumption:** That enough households are willing to let their equipment be controlled for it to make a difference to the grid’s load.

**What we do not know:** How the grid companies’ revenue rules affect whether it pays for them to buy flexibility.

- **Stop if:** participants to a significant extent cancel because of reduced comfort or privacy problems.
- **Adjust if:** the flexibility exists but costs more per kilowatt than reinforcing the grid.
- **Continue if:** the shifted power is sufficient to postpone grid reinforcements, at a lower cost than the reinforcement.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** Three pilot areas with different conditions, for example a suburb, a smaller town and an area with capacity shortage in the grid, over three years.

**Evaluation:** how much power can be moved, the cost per kilowatt compared with grid reinforcement, participants’ experience and how the compensation is distributed.

## Rough cost estimate

| Item | Per pilot and year |
|------|------------------|
| Platform and operation | approx. SEK 0.5–1 million |
| Compensation to participants | depends on how much flexibility is used |
| Evaluation | approx. SEK 0.5 million |

## Legal and practical issues

1. **Grid companies’ incentives:** The Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate’s rules for grid companies’ revenue affect whether it pays for grid companies to buy flexibility instead of expanding. How the rules can promote flexibility needs to be monitored.
2. **Personal data and security:** Control of equipment in homes requires high information security and clear agreements.
3. **Consumer protection:** Participants must be able to terminate the agreement easily and understand what they are agreeing to.

## Objections

**“It benefits those with an electric car and heat pump.”** Yes, primarily. But lower grid costs benefit all customers in the long term.

**“Control of households’ equipment is privacy-sensitive.”** That is why participants themselves need to set the limits and be able to cancel.

## What you can do

**If you work at a grid company:** Map where in the grid flexibility could replace or postpone reinforcements.

**If you own a house:** Investigate whether your electricity agreement can be combined with controlled charging or control of the heat pump.
