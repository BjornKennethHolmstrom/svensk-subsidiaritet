# Municipal safety budgets

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** The municipality allocates a sum per resident for safety measures in areas with high levels of insecurity and lets residents propose and vote on how the money should be used, through so-called participatory budgeting. The proposal is linked to municipalities’ statutory responsibility for crime prevention work since 2023.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

Since July 2023, every municipality must produce a situational picture of crime, decide on an action plan and coordinate local crime prevention work. The law does not say how residents should be involved. Those who live with insecurity often know where it arises and what would help: a dark footpath, a closed youth centre, a place where young people lack adults. Participatory budgeting is a way to make use of that knowledge and at the same time strengthen confidence that the municipality is listening.

## How it would work

1. **A targeted budget** for areas where the municipality’s situational picture shows high insecurity, or which are included in the police’s situational picture of vulnerable areas. Proposal: SEK 100 per resident in the area per year.
2. **Proposal phase:** everyone who lives or works in the area, including young people, can submit proposals. The municipality helps to calculate costs and check that the proposals are legal and feasible.
3. **Voting:** residents vote, digitally and in person at, for example, libraries and schools. Proposal: voting rights from age 16, with the possibility of lowering the age limit.
4. **Implementation:** the proposals that receive the most votes are implemented within the budget.
5. **Follow-up:** a randomly selected group of residents follows up that the money is used as decided, and the result is reported openly.

**Examples of what the money can be used for:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------------------|
| Physical environment | Lighting, clearing of bushes, upgrading of parks and footpaths |
| Young people | Extended opening hours at youth centres, summer jobs, sports and cultural activities |
| Social measures | Safety Rings, community mediators, support for night walks |
| Other | Safety walks, camera surveillance where it is legal and residents want it |

## An important legal limitation

Under the Instrument of Government, decision-making power in municipalities is exercised by elected assemblies. A vote among residents therefore cannot formally bind the municipality. There are two paths:

- **Political commitment:** the municipal council or a committee undertakes in advance to implement the proposals that receive the most votes, within budget and law, and then makes formal decisions accordingly. This is how participatory budgeting works in practice in most places.
- **Legislative change:** an explicit possibility for municipalities to use participatory budgeting, with clear rules. A change giving residents formal decision-making power would likely require more than an amendment to the Local Government Act.

The proposal is based on the first path, which can be tested today.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** 20 municipalities of different sizes, with one to three areas each, over three years.

**Organisation:**
- the funds are allocated in the municipality’s budget and linked to the action plan under the Act on Municipalities’ Responsibility for Crime Prevention Work
- one process facilitator per area (proposal: half-time) who informs, collects proposals, organises the vote and follows up
- a steering group with residents, the municipality and civil society

**Evaluation:**
- participation: how many submit proposals and vote, and which groups participate or do not
- implementation: what proportion of the budget is used as decided
- safety: change in survey responses compared with similar areas
- confidence: change in confidence in the municipality and the police

## Objections

**“Participation will be skewed.”** This is a known weakness of participatory budgeting: those who are already active participate most, while young people, newcomers and people who work inconvenient hours participate less. Outreach work, voting in several languages and locations, and special efforts to reach young people need to be included from the start.

**“The proposals will be populist or ineffective.”** The municipality’s role is to test the proposals against law and cost, and to share knowledge about what has been shown to work. But the point is also that residents gain influence, even when they choose differently from the experts.

**“It could go to camera surveillance that violates privacy.”** Camera surveillance is regulated by law and in many cases requires a permit. Proposals that do not meet the requirements cannot be implemented.

**“The money is taken from something else.”** Yes, that is a trade-off. One option is to use part of the funds the municipality already spends on crime prevention work.

## Experience to build on

- **Participatory budgeting** has been used since the late 1980s, first in Porto Alegre, Brazil, and later in, among other places, Paris and New York. Several Swedish municipalities have tested it on a smaller scale.
- **Municipalities’ situational pictures and action plans** under the 2023 law provide a natural framework.
- **Safety surveys**, which the police and municipalities conduct in many places, can be used for follow-up.

## What you can do

**If you are a municipal politician:** Propose that part of the municipality’s crime prevention budget be tested with participatory budgeting in one area, and that the municipal council commit in advance to implementing the result.

**If you are a civil servant:** Propose a small pilot in which residents can choose between a few proposals, and evaluate after one year.

**If you live in an area with high insecurity:** Ask the municipality how residents were able to take part in producing the situational picture and action plan, and propose that part of the budget be decided by residents.
