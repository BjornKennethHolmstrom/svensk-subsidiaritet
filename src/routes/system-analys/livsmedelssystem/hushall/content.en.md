# The role of households

[← Back to the main article](..)

**Summary:** Households that can cook from scratch, make use of seasonal ingredients and manage on their own for a while make the entire food system more resilient. The proposals concern cooking in school, seasonal adaptation in public kitchens, the opportunity to borrow equipment for preserving, and home preparedness that takes local conditions into account.

*This is a proposal to test in limited pilots with evaluation. The figures below are proposals and rough estimates, not results.*

## Why

During the covid-19 pandemic, among other things yeast and flour temporarily ran out in many stores, because demand increased rapidly when many people started baking at home. This shows how quickly demand can change and how sensitive supply chains are to such changes.

The authorities recommend that households have food, water and other things needed to manage for at least a week in a crisis.

## Proposals

### Cooking in school

Home and consumer studies is already a subject in compulsory school. Schools can cooperate with school kitchens and local producers so that pupils learn more about ingredients, seasons and cooking.

### Seasonal adaptation in public kitchens

Municipalities can plan meals in schools and elderly care according to season, and tell pupils and residents about it. It can also reduce costs and food waste.

### Borrowing equipment for preserving

Libraries and associations can lend equipment for drying, making jam, making juice and fermenting, and study associations can organise courses. The Rural Economy and Agricultural Societies and the study associations have long experience of such advice.

### Home preparedness with a local connection

Municipalities can supplement the authorities’ general advice with information about which foods can be stored and which are produced in the local area.

## Pilot proposal

**Scope:** A few municipalities testing seasonal planning in public kitchens and lending of equipment via libraries over two years.

**Evaluation:** costs and food waste in the public kitchens, how many borrow equipment and take part in courses, and how households experience their own preparedness.

## Objections

**“People do not have time to cook from scratch.”** The aim is not for everyone to do it every day, but for more people to be able to do it when needed.

**“It is moralising.”** The proposals are about offering knowledge and opportunities, not about controlling what people eat.
