# What We Know

## Claims the corpus currently supports with reasonable confidence

This page presents only claims for which the evidence currently supports a reasonably confident synthesis — not everything we suspect, hope, or find plausible. Each claim states its scope and confidence explicitly. See the full picture, including everything *not* listed here, in the **[Evidence Matrix →](/evidens/syntes/matris/)**.

---

### C1 — Governance fit

> **Current evidence is more consistent with a conditional governance-fit proposition than with a universal decentralisation principle.**

**Scope:** Swedish territorial and administrative reforms studied so far (municipal mergers, school decentralization, elder care, fiscal equalization, intermunicipal cooperation, the rescue service's organisational forms).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/)–[S6](/evidens/fall/sverige/raddningstjanst/), hypotheses H3, H5, H7, H8, H9, H14, H21.
**Qualification:** This is a pattern across six cases in one country's administrative history — not yet a cross-national or cross-domain generalization. S6 is the first outcome test within a function, but its two branches measure different things, so it does not raise confidence.

---

### C2 — Formal decentralization is an insufficient proxy for subsidiarity

> **Formal transfer of responsibility does not reliably establish substantive decision proximity.**

**Scope:** Cases where formal responsibility moved (school municipalization, municipal mergers).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), S2, hypotheses H4, H10.
**Qualification:** We have not yet completed a full authority/information/resource mapping below the formal administrative level for any case — this claim rests on partial reconstruction.

---

### C3 — Functional differentiation matters

> **The appropriate governance scale appears to vary substantially by function.**

**Scope:** Swedish municipal functions examined in S1, S3, S4, S5.
**Confidence:** Medium–High.
**Supporting evidence:** [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), S3, S4, S5, hypotheses H5, H7, H14.
**Qualification:** This is one of our best-supported claims — even the state's own 1961 reform documents explicitly recognized different functional scale requirements — but it still needs systematic cross-sector comparison rather than case-by-case observation.

---

### C4a — Governance functions can be factorized across scales

> **Different components of a governance function — political authority, production, specialist capacity, financing, and coordination — can sit at different scales rather than being bundled at a single territorial level.**

**Scope:** Comparison of merger (S1) against cooperation (S5) and equalization (S4), plus the sixteen functions component-coded across the two [governance factorization tests](/evidens/datamodeller/simuleringar/styrningsfaktorisering/) — eight municipal in v0.1, eight mechanically selected in v0.5 — and the rescue service's cooperation systems in [S6](/evidens/fall/sverige/raddningstjanst/).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** S4, S5, S6, comparison with [S1](/evidens/fall/sverige/kommunsammanslagningar/), hypotheses H8, H9, plus the component coding of IT, procurement, environmental inspection, fire and rescue, water and sewerage, physical planning, elder care, and specialist social services.
**Qualification:** Upgraded from "promising" to conditionally supported after component separation was observed in all eight functions in the v0.1 test, then replicated across eight new functions in v0.5 — now regional and national, not only municipal. The claim is that factorization *occurs*, not that it produces better outcomes. The first outcome test (S6, the rescue service) shows no uniform direction: sometimes joint authority appears to add something beyond cooperation, sometimes it coincides with worse outcomes. Confidence is therefore unchanged.

*The other half of the old C4 — that problem structure suffices to predict **which** factorization is right — is now **C4b** and stands as mixed/unresolved in the [Evidence Matrix](/evidens/syntes/matris/). It doesn't belong on this page yet.*

---

### C5 — Coordination is a design variable, not merely a cost

> **When authority and capacity are distributed across multiple nodes, coordination architecture becomes a critical determinant of whether decentralisation produces benefits or fragmentation.**

**Scope:** Cases involving cross-boundary or cross-principal coordination (S3, S5, S6).
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Supporting evidence:** S3, S5, S6, hypotheses H6, H8, H14, and GAE's formal coordination mechanisms (used as an [IP] interpretive lens).
**Qualification:** GAE's coordination-tax formalism is a model result, not independently empirically validated in these Swedish cases yet. S6 shows that operational coordination and formal authority can be empirically distinguished, but not which of them produces the best outcomes.

---

## What's deliberately absent from this list

Three synthesis claims (C4b, C4c and C6) and twelve hypotheses (H2, H11–H13, H15–H22) are currently **exploratory, unresolved or too weakly supported** for this page — not because they're uninteresting, but because the evidence doesn't yet support confident synthesis. They live in the **[Evidence Matrix](/evidens/syntes/matris/)** and **[What We Don't Know](/evidens/syntes/okanda/)** instead of here.

C4c — that pooled authority is not a monotonic scale step — is the newest example. It has some support in the rescue service results, but rests on two studies that measure different things.

We'd rather have a short, defensible list than a long, overconfident one.
